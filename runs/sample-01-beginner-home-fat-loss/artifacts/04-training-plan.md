# Training Plan

## Summary

A 12-week, three-phase (4 + 4 + 4 weeks) full-body bodyweight strength plan for a sedentary complete beginner
training at home with only a sturdy chair, a towel and a wall. Three sessions a week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday
evenings), each 30 minutes in total: a **20-minute strength block** (3-minute warm-up plus about 14-17 minutes of
circuit training) followed by a **10-minute low-impact cardio block** that is planned separately. Two alternating
workouts (A and B) each train legs (squat pattern), hips (bridge/hinge pattern), horizontal push, horizontal pull and
core, so every major muscle group is trained three times a week with one push and one pull exercise per session (1:1).

- No jumping, running or burpees; every exercise is quiet, floor- or wall-based and fits a mat-sized area.
- Every exercise comes from the vetted exercise pool (dataset ids given). No swaps were needed.
- Dose follows the guidelines: 1 set rising to 2 in Phase 1, 2 rising to 3 in Phase 2, 3 sets at harder variations in
  Phase 3, 8-12 reps, ending sets 2-3 reps short of failure, short rests in circuit format
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf),
  [ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823),
  [Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
- Progression is bodyweight-specific: reps first, then sets, then the next harder variation from the pool, with week 12
  used as a lighter re-test week.

This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

## Details

### Structure

**Length and phases** (from "Recommended Program Length" in 02-guidelines):

| Phase | Weeks | Sets per exercise | Reps / holds | Effort (reps in reserve) | Rest between exercises | Strength block time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | 1-2 | 1 | 8-12 reps (holds 15-20 s) | 3-4 in reserve (technique first) | 60-90 s | warm-up 3 + about 9 min = about 12 min |
| 1. Foundation | 3-4 | 2 | 8-12 reps (holds 15-25 s) | 3 in reserve | 45-60 s | warm-up 3 + about 15 min = about 18 min |
| 2. Build | 5-6 | 2 | 8-12 reps (holds 20-30 s) | 2-3 in reserve | 30-45 s | warm-up 3 + about 13 min = about 16 min |
| 2. Build | 7-8 | 3 | 8-12 reps (holds 15-25 s) | 2-3 in reserve | 20 s (circuit transition) | warm-up 3 + about 16 min = about 19 min |
| 3. Consolidate | 9-11 | 3 | 8-12 reps, 6-10 per side on single-side moves (holds 15-25 s) | 2-3 in reserve | 20 s (circuit transition) | warm-up 3 + about 16.5 min = about 19.5 min |
| 3. Consolidate | 12 (re-test, lighter) | 2 (Mon: 1 re-test set) | as weeks 9-11 | 3-4 in reserve | 45 s | warm-up 3 + about 12 min = about 15 min |

Time estimates assume about 3-4 s per rep (2 s down, 1-2 s up), so 12 reps take about 40-45 s, and single-side
exercises take about twice as long. The block never exceeds 20 minutes. If a session runs over, drop the last round
rather than cutting the warm-up or the cardio block.

**Weekly layout** (Workouts A and B alternate, so each is done three times every two weeks; Monday-Wednesday-Friday
gives about 48 hours between sessions for each muscle group, per
[Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)):

| Day | Odd weeks (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11) | Even weeks (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Workout A | Workout B | 30 min: strength block 20 min (incl. 3 min warm-up) + cardio block 10 min |
| Tuesday | Rest (optional easy walk) | Rest (optional easy walk) | - |
| Wednesday | Workout B | Workout A | 30 min: strength block 20 min (incl. 3 min warm-up) + cardio block 10 min |
| Thursday | Rest (optional easy walk) | Rest (optional easy walk) | - |
| Friday | Workout A | Workout B | 30 min: strength block 20 min (incl. 3 min warm-up) + cardio block 10 min |
| Saturday, Sunday | Rest (optional easy walk) | Rest (optional easy walk) | - |

Strength sessions per week: 3 (matches "Session allocation" 3 / 3 in 01-requirements). The cardio block content and
any optional walking are defined in the cardio plan; the strength block comes first so the warm-up below prepares the
whole session.

**Format:** a circuit. Do one set of each exercise in the listed order, moving on after the rest time; repeat for the
prescribed number of rounds. Exercises alternate lower body, upper body and core, so short rests are practical, and
ACSM 2026 found rest length does not change strength outcomes
([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).

Coverage check (per week, both workouts): legs/knee-dominant 3 sessions, hips/glutes 3, horizontal push 3, horizontal
pull 3, core 3 (anti-extension, lateral and anti-rotation alternate), calves 3 (warm-up). Push and pull sets are equal
in every session, plus an extra upper-back warm-up set, so there is no push/pull imbalance. This meets the "all major
muscle groups at least twice a week" guideline
([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html),
[ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).

## Progression

General rules (all phases):

- **Effort:** finish every set with 2-3 good reps left (Phase 1: 3-4), never to failure
  ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
- **Order of progression:** reps, then sets (on the schedule below), then the next harder variation from the "Easier /
  harder option" column. This applies progressive overload without external load
  ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
- **Rep rule (double progression):** when you complete the top of the range (12 reps, or 10 per side) on **all** sets
  with clean form and at least 2 reps in reserve **in two consecutive sessions of the same workout**, move to the
  harder option next time and restart at the bottom of the range (8 reps, or 6 per side). This matches the "progress
  once 12 reps are comfortable" rule in
  [Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)
  and the pool's progression rule in 03-exercise-selection.
- **Hold rule:** when the top of the hold range is reached on all sets in two consecutive sessions, add 5 s per hold.
  When 30 s is easy, move to the harder option and restart at 15 s.
- **Rate of change:** at most one change per exercise per week (one extra set or one harder variation, never both in
  the same week), in line with "start low and go slow" and step-ups every week or two
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
- **Logging:** write down variation, reps (or seconds) and an effort rating (0-10) for every set; the rules above are
  checked against this log.

### Phase 1: Foundation (weeks 1-4)

- Weeks 1-2: 1 round. Week 3: 2 rounds. Week 4: 2 rounds.
- Start every exercise with the variation listed, at 8 reps (or 15 s). Add 1-2 reps per set each session when the
  last session was completed with 3 or more reps in reserve.
- Variation upgrades are allowed only from week 3 and only with the rep rule.
- **Phase exit test (end of week 4):** 2 sets of 10 or more clean incline push-ups, potty squats with support and towel
  rows, and a 25 s wall sit. If not met, repeat week 4 once (this does not extend the program beyond one week; the
  later phase shortens by one week instead).

### Phase 2: Build (weeks 5-8)

- Weeks 5-6: 2 rounds. Weeks 7-8: 3 rounds.
- Start week 5 at the bottom of each range on the new variations (the first week of a phase acts as a light week).
- Push progression: incline push-up on lower stable surfaces, then kneeling push-up (3211) once 2 x 12 incline
  push-ups at the lowest stable height meet the rep rule.
- Squat, hinge, pull and core: apply the rep and hold rules; Workout A bridge moves to glute bridge march (3561)
  from week 5 if the Phase 1 exit test was passed, otherwise stay on the low glute bridge (3013).
- **Phase exit test (end of week 8):** 3 sets of 10 or more clean potty squats, incline push-ups at a low height (or
  kneeling push-ups), towel rows and hip thrusts on the chair, and a 25 s incline side plank per side.

### Phase 3: Consolidate (weeks 9-12)

- Weeks 9-11: 3 rounds. Week 12: lighter re-test week (below).
- **Entry rule for late-phase items:** split squats (2368) and single leg bridge (3645) are used only if the Phase 2
  exit test was passed with clean form and no knee or back discomfort. Otherwise keep squat to overhead reach (1685)
  and glute bridge march (3561) in Workout B.
- Push: kneeling push-up (3211) is the main push; move to the full push-up (0662) only with the rep rule (3 x 12
  kneeling push-ups in two consecutive sessions).
- Tempo progression: when a variation meets the rep rule and no harder pool variation remains, use 3 s lowering and
  a 1-2 s pause instead of adding exercises.

### Deload and re-test (week 12)

The guidelines place a re-test with lighter load in the final week (02-guidelines, Phase 3).

- Monday: warm-up, then 1 re-test set per exercise of Workout B (even week) at the current variation: maximum clean
  reps stopping at 1-2 reps in reserve, and maximum clean holds. Compare with the week 1 log.
- Wednesday and Friday: 2 rounds instead of 3, 45 s rest, 3-4 reps in reserve.
- After week 12 the routine continues with the Phase 3 sessions (or a new 4-8-week block), in line with changing the
  routine every 4-8 weeks
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).

### Hard sessions, soreness and pain

- **Hard session** (effort 8/10 or more, form breaking down, or reps dropping 3 or more below the last session): repeat
  the same targets next session; do not progress.
- **Two hard sessions in a row, or soreness lasting more than 48 hours:** remove one round for the rest of that week
  (reactive deload), then resume the planned volume the following week.
- **Pain or joint discomfort:** stop that exercise, use the easier option, and consult a health professional before
  progressing it again
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Stop exercising immediately for chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness
  ([British Heart Foundation](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).

### Missed-session policy

- **One missed session:** do not double up. Do the next scheduled session on its normal day and keep the A/B order
  (do the missed workout next). The week still counts if 2 of 3 sessions were done.
- **A whole week missed:** repeat the last fully completed week before moving on.
- **Two or more weeks missed:** restart at the first week of the current phase with one round fewer than planned,
  then follow the normal schedule. Gradual return is consistent with
  [PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)
  guidance that the size of an increase relative to current activity drives injury risk.
- Short on time on a given day: do the warm-up plus 2 rounds rather than skipping the session.
