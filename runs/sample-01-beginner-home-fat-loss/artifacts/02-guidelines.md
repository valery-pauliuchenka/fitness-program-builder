# Evidence-Based Guidelines

## Summary

The user is a sedentary 34-year-old complete beginner who wants to lose about 5 kg over a few months. They train at
home with bodyweight only (plus a chair and towel), 3 x 30 min on Mon/Wed/Fri, using low-impact cardio only. The
evidence points to the following:

- **Activity target:** adults should work toward 150-300 min/week of moderate aerobic activity plus muscle
  strengthening for all major muscle groups on 2 or more days/week
  ([WHO 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity),
  [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html),
  [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).
  The 3 x 30 min schedule (90 min/week) meets the strengthening guideline but falls short of the aerobic minimum.
  Optional extra walking on other days is the sustainable way to close that gap.
- **Strength:** 3 full-body sessions with a rest day in between suit a beginner. Start with 1 set of 8-12 reps per
  exercise and build toward 2-3 sets, stopping about 2-3 reps short of failure. Bodyweight and home training is
  effective ([ACSM 2026 Position Stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823),
  [ACSM 2026 summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)).
- **Cardio:** low-impact work at moderate intensity, meaning you can talk but not sing, or about 5-6 on a 0-10
  effort scale ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)).
- **Pace of progress:** start low and go slow, adding small amounts every week or two
  ([Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  A healthy rate of weight loss is about 0.5-1 kg/week
  ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/overweight-and-obesity/)). Exercise on its own produces only modest
  weight loss ([Jayedi et al. 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2828487)).
- **Recommended length:** 12 weeks in three 4-week phases. This confirms the provisional length.

This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

## Recommended Program Length

**12 weeks** (provisional length confirmed), in three 4-week phases:

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Typical changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | 1-4 | Learn technique, build the habit, let joints and muscles adapt | 1 set of 8-12 reps per exercise, rising to 2 sets by weeks 3-4. Cardio block at light-to-moderate effort (talk test). Easiest exercise variations |
| 2. Build | 5-8 | Add volume and modest intensity | 2 sets, rising to 3. Harder variations once 12 clean reps are comfortable. Cardio block mostly moderate (effort 5-6/10) |
| 3. Consolidate | 9-12 | Progress further and make the routine self-sustaining | 2-3 sets at harder variations, about 2-3 reps in reserve. Cardio block steady moderate, with short brisker intervals that stay low-impact. Final week used for a re-test and lighter load |

Justification:

- **Gradual adaptation:** inactive adults should start with lower intensity and increase gradually. Young adults
  can usually add small amounts every week or two, and less fit adults should progress more slowly
  ([PAG 2nd ed., 2018](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  Three 4-week phases allow two progression steps per phase.
- **Phase length:** a government resistance-training guide advises raising intensity only after 4-6 weeks of
  regular training, then changing the routine every 4-8 weeks
  ([Better Health Channel, Victoria](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Phases of 4 weeks fit inside that window.
- **Matching the goal timeframe:** at the healthy rate of 0.5-1 kg/week
  ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/overweight-and-obesity/);
  [CDC, 1-2 lb/week](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html)), losing about 5 kg takes
  several weeks at the very least. Twelve weeks matches "a few months" and allows for weeks where weight stays flat.
- **Complexity:** the 2026 ACSM stand found that complex periodization was not clearly better than simple
  progressive programs for healthy adults
  ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)). A simple linear 3-phase structure is therefore
  enough.

## Details

### Weekly volume and frequency

- **Aerobic activity:** adults should accumulate 150-300 min/week of moderate activity, 75-150 min/week of vigorous
  activity, or an equivalent mix. Any amount is better than none
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf);
  [ODPHP top 10](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know);
  [WHO fact sheet, 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)).
- **Muscle strengthening:** at least 2 days/week covering legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms
  ([CDC, 2023](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html);
  [NHS, reviewed 2024](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).
  Three full-body sessions a week exceed this minimum.
- **Short bouts count:** the old 10-minute minimum bout was removed, so a 10-minute cardio block counts toward the
  weekly total
  ([ODPHP](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know)).
- **Fat-loss context:**
  - Many people need more than 150 min/week to maintain weight. People losing more than 5% of body weight may need
    more than 300 min/week. Combining activity with reduced calorie intake works better than either alone
    ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  - The NHS suggests building toward 45-60 min of activity a day for weight loss, split into shorter sessions if
    needed ([NHS, reviewed 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/overweight-and-obesity/)).
- **Gap and how to close it:** the scheduled 90 min/week is a sensible starting dose for a sedentary beginner but sits
  below the aerobic target. The program can encourage optional everyday walking on non-training days, increased
  gradually, as recommended by CDC: start slowly and work up toward 150 min
  ([CDC, 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/features/getting-more-active-minutes.html)). Reducing long
  sitting periods is also recommended
  ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).
- **Where the sources disagree on minutes:**
  - WHO and CDC give 150-300 min/week as the general health target.
  - The NHS weight page gives a higher daily figure (45-60 min) for weight loss.
  - PAG gives more than 300 min/week for substantial weight loss.

  All agree that more activity brings more benefit and that people should build up to it.

### Strength training dose

- **Frequency:** train all major muscle groups at least twice weekly
  ([ACSM 2026 Position Stand, MSSE 58(4)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
- **Sets and reps:**
  - One set of 8-12 reps per exercise is effective, and 2-3 sets may be more effective
    ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  - ACSM 2026 cites 2-3 sets per exercise for strength
    ([ACSM summary, 2026](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)).
  - Beginners can start with 1 set of about 8 reps and build toward 2-3 sets of 8-12
    ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
- **Exercise selection:** use about 8-10 exercises across the major muscle groups (same Better Health source). In a
  20-minute block, a smaller full-body selection of about 5-7 exercises, covering every major group across the week,
  is a practical adaptation.
- **Effort:**
  - Training to failure is not needed. Ending sets about 2-3 reps short of failure is suggested
    ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
  - PAG describes effective sets as reaching the point where another rep would be difficult. These fit together if
    "difficult" is read as near-failure, not failure.
- **Equipment:** home-based, bodyweight and elastic-band training produced clear gains in strength and function. A
  gym is not required
  ([ACSM summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/);
  [ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
- **Rest between sets:** short (under 1 min) and longer rest did not change strength outcomes
  ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)). A circuit format with short transitions is
  therefore acceptable within 30 minutes.
- **Individualisation:** programs should match personal goals, enjoyment and safety so they stay sustainable
  ([ACSM summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)). This matches the user's
  "easy to stick to" requirement.

### Cardio dose

- **Intensity:**
  - Moderate means about 5-6 on a 0-10 effort scale: you can talk but not sing.
  - Vigorous means 7-8: only a few words before pausing for breath
    ([CDC, 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)).
  - For a sedentary beginner, Phase 1 should stay at light-to-moderate effort.
- **Choice of activity:** brisk walking is a standard moderate-intensity example
  ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)). Low-impact indoor options such as
  marching, step-ups on a low stable surface and shadow boxing suit the no-jumping, no-running constraint. Note that
  this activity list is applied reasoning, not taken from the sources.
- **Dose and effect:**
  - In 116 trials, each extra 30 min/week of aerobic exercise was linked to about 0.5 kg more weight loss.
  - About 150 min/week of moderate-or-higher intensity was associated with clinically important reductions in
    waist size and body fat.
  - Benefits continued to rise up to 300 min/week
    ([Jayedi et al., JAMA Netw Open, Dec 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2828487)).
- **Implication for this schedule:** the 3 x 10-minute in-session cardio block (30 min/week) is a modest dose on its
  own. This supports encouraging optional walking outside the sessions.

### Recovery

- **Rest between sessions:** give each muscle group about 48 hours before training it again
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Monday/Wednesday/Friday full-body sessions provide this naturally.
- **Warm-up and cool-down:** warm up before activity, using lighter versions of the strength exercises, and cool down
  afterwards to bring heart rate and breathing down gradually
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  Better Health suggests about 5 minutes of light aerobic activity plus dynamic stretches
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  In a 30-minute session, the warm-up and cool-down have to fit inside the time budget.
- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours of sleep a night
  ([CDC, 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). CDC also lists enough sleep and stress management
  alongside activity as parts of healthy weight loss
  ([CDC, 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html)).
- **Rest days:** non-training days can include light walking. Light-to-moderate activity added in small amounts
  carries a low risk of musculoskeletal injury
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).

### Progression and rate of change

- **Rate of increase:**
  - Start low and go slow, and increase activity gradually whatever the starting level.
  - The size of each weekly increase relative to current activity drives injury risk.
  - Adding 5-15 min of light-to-moderate activity 2-3 times a week carries low risk.
  - Young adults can usually step up every week or two
    ([PAG 2nd ed., 2018](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
- **Strength progression rule:** progress once the target is comfortably exceeded, for example 2 extra reps or
  12 comfortable reps
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
- **Bodyweight progression:** external load cannot increase, so progress in this order: reps, then sets, then a harder
  variation. Examples of harder variations are a lower incline for push-ups, a slower tempo, or a longer hold. This
  order is an applied adaptation of the "progressive overload" principle, which ACSM 2026 says is needed for
  continued long-term gains
  ([ACSM 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823)).
- **Cardio progression:** increase duration first, then effort, while staying within the talk test
  ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)).
- **Weight change:**
  - A healthy rate is about 0.5-1 kg/week ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/overweight-and-obesity/)), or
    1-2 lb/week, a pace linked to better long-term maintenance
    ([CDC, 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html)).
  - Exercise alone produces modest loss (about 2.8 kg on average at 150 min/week across trials of 8-48 weeks)
    ([Jayedi et al. 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2828487)).
  - The program should therefore present weight loss as a likely but variable outcome. It should also track fitness
    markers such as reps, sets and walking minutes.
  - The program gives no diet or calorie guidance (out of scope). It can neutrally note that the sources find
    activity plus eating changes works best.

### Limitation-specific safety guidance

No injuries or medical conditions were reported. The relevant constraints are a sedentary or beginner fitness level
and non-medical exclusions (no jumping, running or burpees).

- **Low fitness:** less fit adults have a higher injury risk at a given amount of activity. Slower increases may
  reduce that risk, especially in adults with overweight or obesity
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
  Use the easiest variations and 1-2 sets in Phase 1.
- **Musculoskeletal discomfort:** adverse events in exercise trials were mostly mild-to-moderate musculoskeletal
  symptoms
  ([Jayedi et al. 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2828487)). If you feel
  discomfort or pain, consult a health professional before progressing
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Prioritise technique over reps (same source).
- **Low-impact constraint:** this is a preference and noise constraint, not a medical one. Moderate intensity can be
  reached with walking-type activity
  ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)), so excluding jumping and running does
  not prevent meeting the guidelines.
- **Equipment safety:** using a chair for step-ups or incline push-ups requires a stable, non-slip setup, in line with
  the general advice to use appropriate equipment and safe environments
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).
- **Health changes:** adults who develop chronic conditions or symptoms should be under the care of a health care
  provider
  ([PAG 2nd ed.](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity/media/pdfs/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf)).

### Red flags to stop and seek care

- **Stop exercising immediately** for chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness. Seek urgent medical
  advice if these do not ease quickly with rest
  ([British Heart Foundation, 2024](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  That article is written for people with heart conditions, but the same stop signs apply generally.
- **Talk to a doctor** about chest pain or dizziness during physical activity
  ([NHLBI, 2022](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/physical-activity/risks)).
- **Too breathless to speak more than a few words** means the effort is vigorous
  ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)). A beginner should ease off to a
  conversational pace.
- **Pain:** if pain persists or you feel joint discomfort, stop progressing and consult a health professional
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
