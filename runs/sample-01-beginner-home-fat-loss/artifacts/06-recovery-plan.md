# Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery Plan

## Summary

- **Schedule it fits:** 3 sessions a week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday evenings), each capped at 30 minutes: a 20-minute
  strength block that starts with the 3-minute warm-up already set in the training plan, then a 10-minute low-impact
  cardio block that starts and ends with 1 minute of easy marching. Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday are rest
  days. Optional walking on those days is described in the cardio plan.
- **Warm-up:** the training plan's 3-minute movement warm-up runs every session. In weeks where the strength block is
  short enough, add a 2-minute pulse raiser first, for 5 minutes of warm-up inside the cap. In weeks 7-11 the strength
  block is full (about 19-19.5 min), so the pulse raiser is optional and done before the session clock starts, never
  instead of a round.
- **Cool-down:** the cardio block's last minute of easy marching is the cool-down that always happens. A short floor
  stretch (2-5 min) fits inside the 30 minutes in weeks 1-2, 5-6 and 12. In the other weeks it is optional, after the
  session.
- **Rest days:** at least one rest day between sessions gives each muscle group about 48 hours. Optional easy walking
  (cardio plan) plus an optional 5-8 minute mobility routine, recommended at least twice a week, aimed at a desk-based
  day.
- **Deloads and missed sessions:** the same rules as the training plan. Week 12 is the lighter re-test week, the first
  week of each phase starts at the bottom of the ranges, there is a reactive deload after hard sessions, and the
  training plan's missed-session policy applies. After a missed session the cardio block repeats the same cardio step
  next time rather than skipping ahead, as the cardio plan says.
- **Habits:** 7 or more hours of sleep, a regular bedtime even on training evenings, fewer long spells of sitting, and
  stress management. No diet, calorie or supplement advice: a registered dietitian is the right source for nutrition.
- **Limitations:** none reported. Care is aimed at the beginner and sedentary starting point and the no-jumping,
  no-running, no-burpees constraint.
- **Red flags:** stop and seek professional advice for chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, sharp pain,
  swelling and the other signs listed below.

This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

## Details

### Warm-up

Warming up before strength work is recommended. Better Health suggests about 5 minutes of light aerobic activity plus
slow, controlled dynamic movements
([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
The BHF also stresses warming up when you start, so that you are still "very light and easy to talk" at the end of it
([BHF aerobic exercises](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/aerobic-exercises)).
Starting each session at a slower pace lets the body prepare
([BHF safe limits](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).

**Part 1: pulse raiser (2 min, added when the time table below allows).** None of these moves is used in the main
strength block, so the training plan's rule that warm-up moves are not repeated in the main block still holds.

| Move | Time | How | Quiet / space note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy march on the spot with arm swings | 45 s | Lift the knees a little, swing the arms naturally, breathe easily | Place the feet down, no stamping; mat-sized area |
| Arm circles (forward then backward) while marching gently | 30 s | Small circles growing to medium size, shoulders relaxed | Stand clear of furniture |
| Hip circles, hands on the hips | 20 s | Slow circles each way, feet planted | Both feet stay on the floor |
| Gentle torso turns | 25 s | Turn the shoulders side to side, hips facing forward, slow and controlled | No bouncing at the end of the turn |

**Part 2: the training plan's movement warm-up (3 min, every session, inside the 20-minute strength block).** Half
knee bends 1 x 12, wall push-up 1 x 10, elbow lift (reverse push-up) 1 x 8 and standing calf raise 1 x 12, exactly as
listed in the training plan. Do not change it here. It warms up the squat, push, upper-back and calf patterns used in
the session.

**Part 3: the cardio block's first minute of easy marching (inside the 10-minute cardio block).** The cardio block
follows the strength block, so you are already warm. This minute is the step from strength work into rhythmic cardio.
The cardio moves themselves (wall march with high knees, back-and-forth step, shadow boxing hooks and straight punches
over a march, V step, and the Phase 3 slow mountain climber on Friday) are set in the cardio plan and are not repeated
in this warm-up or cool-down. The pulse raiser's arm circles and torso turns are gentle range-of-motion moves, not
punches, and its easy march is the same easy pace the cardio block uses for its lead-in and cool-down minutes.

**Time table (the 30-minute cap always wins).** Strength block times are taken from the training plan. Extras are
dropped first if a session runs long. After that, follow the training plan's rule of dropping the last round rather
than cutting the 3-minute warm-up or the cardio block.

| Weeks | Strength block (incl. 3-min warm-up) | Pulse raiser (2 min) | Cardio block | In-session stretch | Session total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | about 12 min | inside the session | 10 min | 5-min full routine | about 29 min |
| 3-4 | about 18 min | inside the session | 10 min | optional, after the session | about 30 min |
| 5-6 | about 16 min | inside the session | 10 min | 2-min short routine | about 30 min |
| 7-8 | about 19 min | optional, before the clock starts | 10 min | optional, after the session | about 29 min |
| 9-11 | about 19.5 min | optional, before the clock starts | 10 min | optional, after the session | about 29.5 min |
| 12 (lighter re-test week) | about 15 min | inside the session | 10 min | 3-min routine | about 30 min |

### Cool-down and mobility

**Always (inside the session):** the last minute of the cardio block is an easy march that brings heart rate and
breathing down gradually. This follows the advice to finish at a slower pace and to march lightly to bring heart rate
down after exercise
([BHF aerobic exercises](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/aerobic-exercises);
[BHF safe limits](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).

**Post-session floor stretch.** This is the NHS after-exercise routine, which takes about 5 minutes with each stretch
held for 15-20 seconds
([NHS: how to stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)). It
fits on a mat or towel. Stretch to mild tension only, hold still and breathe normally. Use it as the time table
allows: inside the session in weeks 1-2, 5-6 and 12, optional after the session in the other weeks.

| Stretch | Hold | How (short version) | 5-min full | 3-min (wk 12) | 2-min short (wks 5-6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calf stretch | 15-20 s per side | Step one leg forward with the knee bent, back leg straight, heel towards the floor; hand on the wall or chair back for support | yes | yes | yes |
| Buttock stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, cross one leg over the other thigh and draw the supporting thigh towards the chest | yes | yes | yes |
| Hamstring stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, one foot on the floor, hold the other straight leg below the knee and draw it gently towards you | yes | yes | no |
| Thigh stretch | 15-20 s per side | Lying on your side, hold the top foot and draw the heel towards the buttock, knees together | yes | no | no |
| Inner thigh stretch | 15-20 s | Sit tall, soles of the feet together, let the knees lower gently | yes | no | no |

These stretches target the calves, glutes, hamstrings and thighs, which do most of the squat, bridge, marching and
stepping work in this program.

**Rest-day mobility (optional, about 5-8 minutes, for example Tuesday and Thursday, after the optional walk or as a
desk break).** NHS flexibility exercises, done at least twice a week, building up slowly
([NHS flexibility exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)):

| Exercise | Dose | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Neck rotation (seated, shoulders down, turn the head slowly towards each shoulder) | hold 5 s, 3 per side | Eases stiffness from desk work |
| Neck stretch (seated, hold one shoulder down, tilt the head away) | hold 5 s, 3 per side | Same |
| Sideways bend (standing, slide one hand down the side of the leg) | hold 2 s, 3 per side | Gentle trunk mobility |
| Calf stretch at the wall (back leg straight, heel down) | 3 per side | Calves work in every session (calf raises, wall march, stepping) |
| Any 2-3 of the post-session floor stretches above | 15-20 s each | Hips and legs after a day of sitting |

This is not a training session and it does not count toward the 3 weekly sessions. Skipping it never counts as a
missed session.

### Rest days, deloads and missed sessions

**Rest-day placement.** Monday, Wednesday and Friday sessions leave at least one rest day between sessions, which gives
each muscle group about 48 hours, the rule of thumb in
[Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits).
Never move two sessions onto back-to-back days to catch up.

| Day | Plan | Recovery focus |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Session (strength block + cardio block) | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Tuesday | Rest | Optional easy walk (cardio plan ladder), optional mobility |
| Wednesday | Session | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Thursday | Rest | Optional easy walk, optional mobility |
| Friday | Session | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Saturday | Rest | Optional walk; a good day for a longer, relaxed walk |
| Sunday | Full rest or an easy walk | Check the training log and plan the coming week |

Rest days can include light activity. Light-to-moderate activity added in small amounts carries a low injury risk
(PAG, as summarised in the guidelines). The cardio plan's optional walking ladder is the only extra activity suggested,
and it stays optional. Breaking up long spells of sitting on rest days and workdays is recommended
([NHS adult guidelines](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).

**Planned lighter weeks (consistent with the training and cardio plans).**

- Weeks 5 and 9 (first week of a phase) start at the bottom of each range on new variations, which works as a light
  week.
- Week 12 is the deload and re-test week. Strength: Monday is 1 re-test set per exercise, and Wednesday and Friday are
  2 rounds with 45 s rest and 3-4 reps in reserve. Cardio: Monday and Wednesday are steady at 5/10 with no bursts, and
  Friday is the re-test. Use the shorter strength block to do the 3-minute in-session stretch.
- No extra deload weeks are added beyond these. Changing the plan's structure is up to the training and cardio plans.

**Reactive deload (from the training plan).** After a hard session (effort 8/10 or more, form breaking down, or reps
3 or more below last time), repeat the same targets. After two hard sessions in a row, or soreness lasting more than
48 hours, remove one round for the rest of that week, then return to the planned volume. In the cardio block on those
days, slow down or make the moves smaller
([BHF aerobic exercises](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/aerobic-exercises)).
Keep the warm-up and the cool-down march as normal.

**Missed sessions (the training plan's policy, applied to the whole session).**

- One missed session: do not double up. Do the next session on its normal day and do the missed workout next (keep
  the A/B order). The week still counts if 2 of 3 sessions were done. The cardio block repeats the same cardio step
  next time rather than skipping ahead, as set in the cardio plan's progression rules.
- A whole week missed: repeat the last fully completed week, for both the strength and cardio blocks.
- Two or more weeks missed: restart at the first week of the current phase with one round fewer than planned. Use the
  first-week cardio routine of the current phase.
- Short on time: do the 3-minute warm-up plus 2 rounds rather than skipping. Keep the cardio block's easy cool-down
  minute if any cardio is done.
- Feeling unwell (for example a fever or a heavy cold): skip the session and treat it as missed under the rules above.
  Do not make it up later in the week.

### Sleep and recovery habits

- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours a night
  ([CDC About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). CDC habits that help:
  - go to bed and get up at the same time every day, including after training evenings
  - keep the bedroom quiet, relaxing and cool
  - turn off screens at least 30 minutes before bed
  - avoid caffeine in the afternoon and evening, and large meals and alcohol before bed
  ([CDC About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)).
- **Evening sessions:** the 30-minute session plus its easy cool-down minute ends at a gentle pace. Keep the same
  bedtime on training days. Note in your log if sleep seems affected and adjust the session start time if you can.
- **Stress and tracking:** CDC lists sleep and healthy ways of reducing stress as part of a healthy weight-loss
  lifestyle, and suggests tracking hours of sleep
  ([CDC Steps for Losing Weight](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html)). Add sleep hours
  and a 0-10 energy rating to the training log the plan already asks for.
- **Move more, sit less:** reduce time spent sitting and break up long periods of not moving
  ([NHS adult guidelines](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).
  On workdays, stand up and walk for a minute or two during long spells at the desk, or do one of the neck or calf
  mobility moves above.
- **Comfort:** wear loose, comfortable clothing and keep water nearby during sessions and mobility work
  ([NHS flexibility exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)).
- **Nutrition:** this plan gives no diet plans, calorie targets or supplement advice. Eating changes affect fat loss,
  and a registered dietitian (your health care provider can refer you) is the right source for that advice
  ([CDC Steps for Losing Weight](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html)).

### Limitation-specific care

No injuries, medical conditions or current pain were reported. The relevant constraints are the starting point
(complete beginner, sedentary desk job) and non-medical exclusions (no jumping, no running, no burpees, low impact
only). Care for each:

- **Beginner and sedentary starting point.**
  - Keep the full warm-up every time, even when short on time. Start and finish at an easy pace
    ([BHF safe limits](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  - Stretches and mobility are gentle: build up slowly
    ([NHS flexibility exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)) and stop at mild
    tension, never pain.
  - If you feel discomfort, check with a health professional before continuing the program
    ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  - Use the reactive-deload rule rather than pushing through. Soreness lasting more than 48 hours is the cue.
- **No jumping, no running, no burpees, low impact.**
  - Every warm-up, cool-down and mobility move keeps at least one foot on the floor or is done lying or seated. There
    are no hops, skips or jogging on the spot.
  - Marching is placed, not stamped. If it gets louder, make the step smaller, not faster (as in the cardio plan).
- **Desk-based day (neck, hips, calves).**
  - Rest-day neck rotations and stretches, the sideways bend, and the hip and calf stretches are aimed at the
    stiffness that long sitting tends to cause.
  - Break up sitting time during the workday
    ([NHS adult guidelines](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/)).
- **Small flat, floor work.**
  - Do floor stretches on a mat or folded towel. Put the towel under the knees for kneeling moves, and under the elbow
    for side planks, as the training plan already says.
  - Use the chair only against a wall so it cannot slide, and use a wall or the chair back for balance in the calf
    stretch
    ([NHS: how to stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)).
- **If anything changes** (a new injury, a new medical condition, or pain that does not settle): stretches may need
  changing. Ask a physiotherapist or health care professional which ones are right for you
  ([NHS: how to stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)).

### Red flags

**Stop and seek professional advice** if any of these happen during or after a session, a walk or mobility work:

- **Chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness:** stop exercising immediately. If the
  symptoms do not go away quickly with rest, seek urgent medical advice
  ([BHF safe limits](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  If you feel unwell, stop and speak to your doctor
  ([BHF aerobic exercises](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/aerobic-exercises)).
- **Unusual shortness of breath or fatigue:** ease off and slow down
  ([BHF safe limits](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  If it does not settle with rest, stop and seek advice.
- **Sharp pain, or pain in a joint:** stop that exercise and check with a health professional before continuing
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
- **Swelling or bruising after a twist or strain, or difficulty putting weight on a limb:** stop and rest the area.
  - Contact NHS 111 or urgent care for severe or worsening pain, large or spreading swelling, not being able to bear
    weight or walk more than a few steps, or an area that is very stiff or hard to move.
  - Call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the limb looks misshapen, there is numbness, tingling or pins and
    needles, or the skin looks blue or grey or feels cold
  ([NHS sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)).
- **Pain that is no better after a few days of self-care, or is getting worse:** see a GP
  ([NHS sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)). Do not progress the affected
  exercise until you have been advised.
