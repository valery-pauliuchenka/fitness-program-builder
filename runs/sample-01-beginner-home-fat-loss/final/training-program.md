# 12-Week Home Fat-Loss Program for Beginners

For a complete beginner training at home with bodyweight, a sturdy chair and a towel: 3 quiet, low-impact sessions a week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday evenings), 30 minutes each.

## 1. Overview

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Goal | Fat loss (about 5 kg over a few months, no hard deadline), in a way that is easy to stick to |
| Program length | 12 weeks, in three 4-week phases: Foundation, Build, Consolidate |
| Sessions per week | 3 (Monday, Wednesday, Friday evenings) |
| Minutes per session | 30: a 20-minute strength block (including a 3-minute warm-up), then a 10-minute low-impact cardio block |
| Equipment | Your bodyweight, a sturdy chair (always placed against a wall), a towel and a wall |
| Space | About a mat-sized area |

Every session trains your whole body. Two strength workouts (A and B) take turns, and each one covers legs, hips and glutes, a pushing move, a pulling move and your core, so every major muscle group is trained three times a week. That is more than the "at least twice a week" minimum in the guidelines [2][3][6]. After the strength block comes a 10-minute cardio block of marching, stepping and shadow boxing. Nothing involves running, jumping or burpees, and one foot always stays on the floor, so it stays quiet for the neighbours below.

Why 12 weeks: people who have been inactive should start gently and build up in small steps every week or two [4]. A resistance-training guide advises raising intensity only after 4-6 weeks of regular training and changing the routine every 4-8 weeks [13], so 4-week phases fit well. A healthy rate of weight loss is about 0.5-1 kg a week [10][11], so about 5 kg needs several weeks at the very least. Twelve weeks matches "a few months" and leaves room for weeks when your weight stays flat. Simple, steady progress works as well as more complicated plans for healthy adults [6].

The 3 x 30 minutes in this program (90 minutes a week) meet the muscle-strengthening guideline, but they are below the 150-300 minutes a week of moderate activity that adults should work toward [1][2][5]. An optional everyday walking plan (section 7) helps you close that gap step by step.

## 2. Safety Notice

This program is general exercise information, not medical advice. You reported no injuries, no medical conditions, no current pain and no restrictions from a doctor, so no medical adaptations were needed. The program is built around your starting point instead:

- **Beginner and mostly seated day:** the easiest exercise versions, 1 set per exercise in the first two weeks, gentle effort, and small steps up. Less fit adults have a higher injury risk at a given amount of activity, and slower increases may reduce that risk [4].
- **No jumping, no running, no burpees, low impact only:** every move keeps at least one foot on the floor, or is done lying, kneeling or seated.
- **Chair safety:** always put the chair against a wall so it cannot slide before using it for push-ups, hip thrusts or the mountain climber [4].
- **Nutrition:** this program contains no diet plans, calorie targets or supplement advice. For advice on eating, a registered dietitian is the right source (your health care provider can refer you) [11].

**Stop and seek professional advice** if any of these happen during or after a session, a walk or mobility work:

- **Chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness:** stop exercising immediately. If the symptoms do not go away quickly with rest, seek urgent medical advice [15]. If you feel unwell, stop and speak to your doctor [17]. Talk to a doctor about any chest pain or dizziness during activity [16].
- **Unusual shortness of breath or tiredness:** ease off and slow down [15]. If it does not settle with rest, stop and seek advice. If you can only say a few words, you are working at vigorous effort; slow down to a pace where you can talk [8].
- **Sharp pain, or pain in a joint:** stop that exercise, use the easier option, and check with a health professional before progressing it again [13].
- **Swelling or bruising after a twist or strain, or difficulty putting weight on a limb:** stop and rest the area.
  - Contact NHS 111 or urgent care for severe or worsening pain, large or spreading swelling, not being able to bear weight or walk more than a few steps, or an area that is very stiff or hard to move.
  - Call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the limb looks misshapen, there is numbness, tingling or pins and needles, or the skin looks blue or grey or feels cold [20].
- **Pain that is no better after a few days of self-care, or is getting worse:** see a GP [20], and do not progress the affected exercise until you have been advised.
- **If your health changes** (a new injury, a new medical condition, or symptoms that do not settle): see a health care provider before continuing [4], and ask a physiotherapist which stretches are right for you [18].
- **Feeling unwell** (for example a fever or a heavy cold): skip the session and treat it as missed (section 7).

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Intensity guidance | Key changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | 1-4 | Learn technique, build the habit, let joints and muscles adapt | Strength: stop each set with 3-4 reps left (weeks 1-2), then 3 left (weeks 3-4). Cardio: light to moderate, 4-5 out of 10, talking easily | Weeks 1-2: 1 round of each workout; weeks 3-4: 2 rounds. Easiest exercise versions. Cardio in short intervals (30 s work / 30 s easy, then 40 s / 20 s) |
| 2. Build | 5-8 | Add volume and modest intensity | Strength: 2-3 reps left. Cardio: moderate, 5 then 5-6 out of 10 (you can talk but not sing) | Weeks 5-6: 2 rounds; weeks 7-8: 3 rounds. | 3. Consolidate | 9-12 | Progress further and make the routine your own | Strength: 2-3 reps left (week 12: 3-4). Week 12 is a lighter week with a re-test |

Effort in this program uses a 0-10 scale: 0 is sitting still, 10 is all-out. "Moderate" is 5-6, where you can talk but not sing [8]. "Reps left" means how many more good repetitions you could still do when you stop a set; you never train to failure [6].

## 5. Weekly Schedule

The weekly layout is the same in all 12 weeks. Workouts A and B take turns, so each is done three times every two weeks:

| Day | Session | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Strength (odd weeks: Workout A; even weeks: Workout B) + low-impact cardio block | 30 min |
| Tuesday | Rest. Optional easy walk and optional 5-8 minute mobility routine | - |
| Wednesday | Strength (odd weeks: Workout B; even weeks: Workout A) + low-impact cardio block | 30 min |
| Thursday | Rest. Optional easy walk and optional 5-8 minute mobility routine | - |
| Friday | Strength (odd weeks: Workout A; even weeks: Workout B) + low-impact cardio block | 30 min |
| Saturday | Rest. Optional walk; a good day for a longer, relaxed walk | - |
| Sunday | Full rest or an easy walk. Check your training log and plan the coming week | - |

Monday-Wednesday-Friday leaves at least one rest day between sessions, which gives each muscle group about 48 hours to recover [13]. Never move two sessions onto back-to-back days to catch up.

**Inside each 30-minute session:** an optional 2-minute pulse raiser (when time allows), the strength block (3-minute warm-up plus a circuit), the 10-minute cardio block (1 minute easy march, 8 minutes of work, 1 minute easy march cool-down), and a short floor stretch when time allows. How the 30 minutes are filled changes by phase as the strength block grows:

| Weeks | Strength block (incl. 3-min warm-up) | Pulse raiser (2 min) | Cardio block | Floor stretch | Session total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | about 12 min | inside the session | 10 min | 5-min full routine | about 29 min |
| 3-4 | about 18 min | inside the session | 10 min | optional, after the session | about 30 min |
| 5-6 | about 16 min | inside the session | 10 min | 2-min short routine | about 30 min |
| 7-8 | about 19 min | optional, before you start the clock | 10 min | optional, after the session | about 29 min |

The 30-minute limit always wins. If a session runs long, drop the extras first (pulse raiser, stretch), then drop the last strength round. Never cut the 3-minute warm-up or the cardio block.

## 6. Workouts

**How the strength block works:** it is a circuit. Do one set of each exercise in the order listed, rest for the listed time, move to the next exercise, and repeat for the number of rounds (sets) shown. Exercises alternate lower body, upper body and core, so short rests are practical; rest length does not change strength results [6]. Each repetition takes about 3-4 seconds (2 s down, 1-2 s up). Numbers in brackets after an exercise name are exercise dataset ids (see section 10).

## 7. Progression Rules

### Strength: general rules (all phases)

- **Effort:** finish every set with 2-3 good reps left (Phase 1: 3-4), never to failure [6].
- **Order of progression:** reps first, then sets (on the schedule in section 6), then the next harder version from the "Easier / harder option" column. This is how you keep making progress without weights [6].
- **Rep rule:** when you complete the top of the range (12 reps, or 10 per side) on **all** sets, with clean form and at least 2 reps left, **in two sessions of the same workout in a row**, move to the harder option next time and restart at the bottom of the range (8 reps, or 6 per side). This matches the advice to progress once 12 reps are comfortable [13].
- **Hold rule:** when you reach the top of the hold range on all sets in two sessions in a row, add 5 s per hold. When 30 s is easy, move to the harder option and restart at 15 s.
- **One change at a time:** at most one change per exercise per week (one extra set or one harder version, never both in the same week). Start low and go slow, stepping up every week or two [4].
- **Log every set** (section 9). These rules are checked against your log.

### Phase 1: Foundation (weeks 1-4)

- Weeks 1-2: 1 round. Weeks 3-4: 2 rounds.
- Start every exercise with the version listed, at 8 reps (or 15 s). Add 1-2 reps per set each session if the last session ended with 3 or more reps left.
- Switching to a harder version is allowed only from week 3, and only with the rep rule.
- **Exit test (end of week 4):** 2 sets of 10 or more clean incline push-ups, supported potty squats and towel rows, plus a 25 s wall sit. If you do not pass, repeat week 4 once. The program does not get longer: the next phase is one week shorter instead.

### Phase 2: Build (weeks 5-8)

- Weeks 5-6: 2 rounds. Weeks 7-8: 3 rounds.
- Start week 5 at the bottom of each range on the new versions. The first week of a phase works as a light week.
- Push-ups: move the hands to lower stable surfaces, then switch to kneeling push-ups (3211) once 2 x 12 incline push-ups at the lowest stable height meet the rep rule.
- Squat, bridge, row and core: use the rep and hold rules. In Workout A, the bridge becomes the glute bridge march (3561) from week 5 if you passed the Phase 1 exit test; otherwise stay on the low glute bridge (3013).
- **Exit test (end of week 8):** 3 sets of 10 or more clean potty squats, low incline push-ups (or kneeling push-ups), towel rows and hip thrusts on the chair, plus a 25 s incline side plank per side.

### Phase 3: Consolidate (weeks 9-12)

- Weeks 9-11: 3 rounds. Week 12: lighter re-test week (below).
- **Entry rule for the hardest moves:** use split squats (2368) and the single leg bridge (3645) only if you passed the Phase 2 exit test with clean form and no knee or back discomfort. Otherwise keep the squat to overhead reach (1685) and the glute bridge march (3561) in Workout B.
- Push-ups: kneeling push-ups (3211) are the main push. Move to full push-ups (0662) only with the rep rule (3 x 12 kneeling push-ups in two sessions in a row).
- When an exercise meets the rep rule and there is no harder version left, slow it down instead: 3 s lowering and a 1-2 s pause. Do not add exercises.

### Lighter week and re-test (week 12)

- **Monday:** warm-up, then 1 re-test set of each Workout B exercise at your current version: as many clean reps as you can while still keeping 1-2 reps left, and your longest clean holds. Compare with your week 1 log.
- **Wednesday and Friday:** 2 rounds instead of 3, 45 s rest, 3-4 reps left.
- **Cardio:** Monday and Wednesday are steady at 5/10 with no bursts; Friday is the cardio re-test (section 9).

### Cardio progression

| Phase | Weeks | Scheduled time | Sessions | Effort | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | 1-2 | 3 x 10 min = 30 min/week (4 min work per block) | 3/week (Mon, Wed, Fri) | 4-5/10 work, 2-3/10 easy march | 30 s work / 30 s easy march x 8 |
| 1. Foundation | 3-4 | 30 min/week (about 5 min work per block) | 3/week | 4-5/10 | 40 s work / 20 s easy march x 8 |
| 2. Build | 5-6 | 30 min/week (8 min continuous work) | 3/week | 5/10 | Non-stop, switch move each minute |
| 2. Build | 7-8 | 30 min/week (8 min continuous work) | 3/week | 5-6/10 | Quicker tempo, bigger range |
| 3. Consolidate | 9-10 | 30 min/week | 3/week | 5-6/10 base, 4 x 20 s bursts at 6-7/10 | Bursts added |
| 3. Consolidate | 11 | 30 min/week | 3/week | 5-6/10 base, 6 x 30 s bursts at 6-7/10 | More and longer bursts |
| 3. Consolidate | 12 | 30 min/week | 3/week | 5/10 steady; Friday re-test | Lighter week |

- Move to the next step only if the current step felt no harder than its target effort and you could still talk in every work interval in the last 2 sessions. Otherwise repeat the step for another week. Taking it slower is fine [12].
- Progress one thing at a time: first fewer breaks (Phase 1 to 2), then tempo and range (Phase 2), then short bursts (Phase 3). Duration comes first, then effort, always within the talk test [8].
- If a session feels too hard, slow down or make the movements smaller [17].

### Optional everyday walking

Not scheduled and not a training session. Do it on rest days or as extra on training days. Brisk walking is a standard moderate activity [8]. Start slowly and add a few minutes at a time [12]; walks of any length count toward the weekly total [5].

| Phase | Optional walking | Effort | Weekly total including the 30 scheduled minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (weeks 1-4) | 10 min on 2 days | 3-5/10, easy chat | about 50 min |
| 2 (weeks 5-8) | 15-20 min on 3 days | 5/10, brisk, talk but not sing | about 75-90 min |
| 3 (weeks 9-12) | 20-30 min on 3-4 days | 5-6/10 | about 90-150 min |

Missing a walk never counts as a missed session. Over time, walking moves your weekly total toward the 150-300 minutes a week guideline [5].

### Hard sessions and soreness

- **Hard session** (effort 8/10 or more, form breaking down, or reps dropping 3 or more below last time): repeat the same targets next session; do not progress.
- **Two hard sessions in a row, or soreness lasting more than 48 hours:** remove one round for the rest of that week, then go back to the planned volume the following week. In the cardio block on those days, slow down or make the moves smaller [17]. Keep the warm-up and the cool-down march as normal.
- **Pain or joint discomfort:** stop that exercise, use the easier option, and check with a health professional before progressing it again [13].

### Missed sessions

- **One missed session:** do not double up. Do the next session on its normal day and do the missed workout next (keep the A/B order). The week still counts if you did 2 of 3 sessions. The cardio block repeats the same cardio step next time rather than skipping ahead.
- **A whole week missed:** repeat the last fully completed week, for both strength and cardio.
- **Two or more weeks missed:** restart at the first week of your current phase with one round fewer than planned, and use the first-week cardio routine of that phase. Returning gradually matters because the size of an increase compared with what you have been doing drives injury risk [4].
- **Short on time:** do the 3-minute warm-up plus 2 rounds rather than skipping. If you do any cardio, keep its easy cool-down minute.
- **Feeling unwell:** skip the session and treat it as missed. Do not make it up later in the week.

## 8. Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery

### Warm-up

A warm-up before strength work is recommended: about 5 minutes of light activity plus slow, controlled movements [13]. You should still feel "very light and easy to talk" at the end of it [17], and starting at a slower pace lets your body prepare [15].

**Part 1: pulse raiser (2 minutes, when the time table in section 5 allows).** None of these moves are used in the main workout.

| Move | Time | How | Quiet / space note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy march on the spot with arm swings | 45 s | Lift the knees a little, swing the arms naturally, breathe easily | Place the feet down, no stamping; mat-sized area |
| Arm circles (forward, then backward) while marching gently | 30 s | Small circles growing to medium size, shoulders relaxed | Stand clear of furniture |
| Hip circles, hands on the hips | 20 s | Slow circles each way, feet planted | Both feet stay on the floor |
| Gentle torso turns | 25 s | Turn the shoulders side to side, hips facing forward, slow and controlled | No bouncing at the end of the turn |

In weeks 7-11 the strength block is full, so the pulse raiser is optional and done before you start the session clock, never instead of a round.

**Part 2: movement warm-up (3 minutes, every session).** Half knee bends, wall push-ups, elbow lifts and calf raises, exactly as listed in section 6. It prepares the squat, push, upper-back and calf patterns used in the session.

**Part 3: the cardio block's first minute of easy marching.** You are already warm from the strength block; this minute is the step from strength work into rhythmic cardio.

### Cool-down

**Always:** the last minute of the cardio block is an easy march that brings your heart rate and breathing down gradually [15][17].

**Floor stretch after the session.** About 5 minutes, each stretch held for 15-20 seconds [18]. Do it on a mat or folded towel. Stretch to mild tension only, hold still and breathe normally. It fits inside the session in weeks 1-2 (full routine), 5-6 (short routine) and 12 (3-minute routine); in the other weeks it is optional, after the session.

| Stretch | Hold | How | 5-min full | 3-min (week 12) | 2-min short (weeks 5-6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calf stretch | 15-20 s per side | Step one leg forward with the knee bent, back leg straight, heel towards the floor; hand on the wall or chair back for support | yes | yes | yes |
| Buttock stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, cross one leg over the other thigh and draw the supporting thigh towards the chest | yes | yes | yes |
| Hamstring stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, one foot on the floor, hold the other straight leg below the knee and draw it gently towards you | yes | yes | no |
| Thigh stretch | 15-20 s per side | Lying on your side, hold the top foot and draw the heel towards the buttock, knees together | yes | no | no |
| Inner thigh stretch | 15-20 s | Sit tall, soles of the feet together, let the knees lower gently | yes | no | no |

These stretches target the calves, glutes, hamstrings and thighs, which do most of the squatting, bridging, marching and stepping in this program.

### Rest-day mobility (optional, about 5-8 minutes)

For example on Tuesday and Thursday, after an optional walk or as a break from the desk. Do these at least twice a week and build up slowly [19].

| Exercise | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Neck rotation (seated, shoulders down, turn the head slowly towards each shoulder) | hold 5 s, 3 per side | Eases stiffness from desk work |
| Neck stretch (seated, hold one shoulder down, tilt the head away) | hold 5 s, 3 per side | Same |
| Sideways bend (standing, slide one hand down the side of the leg) | hold 2 s, 3 per side | Gentle trunk mobility |
| Calf stretch at the wall (back leg straight, heel down) | 3 per side | Calves work in every session (calf raises, wall march, stepping) |
| Any 2-3 of the floor stretches above | 15-20 s each | Hips and legs after a day of sitting |

This is not a training session, and skipping it never counts as a missed session.

### Rest days

- Rest days can include light activity: small amounts of light-to-moderate activity carry a low risk of injury [4]. The optional walking plan (section 7) is the only extra activity suggested.
- Break up long spells of sitting on rest days and workdays [3]. On workdays, stand up and walk for a minute or two during long spells at the desk, or do one of the neck or calf mobility moves.
- Planned lighter weeks: the first week of each new phase starts at the bottom of the ranges, and week 12 is the lighter re-test week. No other lighter weeks are added; use the hard-session rules in section 7 when you need extra recovery.

### Sleep and everyday habits

- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours a night [14]. Habits that help [14]:
  - go to bed and get up at the same time every day, including after training evenings
  - keep the bedroom quiet, relaxing and cool
  - turn off screens at least 30 minutes before bed
  - avoid caffeine in the afternoon and evening, and large meals and alcohol before bed
- **Evening sessions:** the session ends at a gentle pace. Keep the same bedtime on training days. Note in your log if your sleep seems affected, and adjust the session start time if you can.
- **Stress:** enough sleep and healthy ways of reducing stress are part of a healthy weight-loss lifestyle [11].
- **Comfort:** wear loose, comfortable clothing and keep water nearby during sessions and mobility work [19].

### Care for your starting point

- **Beginner and mostly seated day:** keep the full warm-up every time, even when short on time, and start and finish at an easy pace [15]. Stretches are gentle: build up slowly and stop at mild tension, never pain [19]. If you feel discomfort, check with a health professional before continuing [13]. Use the hard-session rule rather than pushing through; soreness lasting more than 48 hours is the cue.
- **No jumping, running or burpees:** every warm-up, cool-down and mobility move keeps at least one foot on the floor or is done lying or seated. No hops, skips or jogging on the spot. March by placing your feet, not stamping.
- **Small flat, floor work:** use a mat or folded towel for floor stretches, the towel under your knees for kneeling moves and under your elbow for side planks. Use the chair only against a wall, and use the wall or chair back for balance in the calf stretch [18].

## 9. Tracking and When to Adjust

### What to log

- **Every strength set:** the exercise version, reps (or seconds) and an effort rating from 0 to 10. The progression rules in section 7 are checked against this log.
- **Every cardio block:** the step you did and your effort rating, and whether you could still talk in every work interval.
- **Optional walks:** minutes and effort.
- **Daily:** hours of sleep [11] and a 0-10 energy rating.
- **Sunday:** review your log and plan the coming week.

### How to judge progress

- **Exit tests** at the end of weeks 4 and 8 (section 7) tell you whether you are ready for the next phase.
- **Week 12 strength re-test (Monday):** compare your best clean reps and holds with week 1.
- **Week 12 cardio re-test (Friday):** repeat the week 3 Friday routine (40 s work / 20 s easy march), then rate the effort and note whether you could talk through it. A lower rating than in week 3 for the same routine shows improved fitness. Also note whether you can now do 8 minutes non-stop at 5-6/10.
- **Weight:** a healthy rate of loss is about 0.5-1 kg a week [10][11], and weeks where your weight stays flat are normal. Exercise on its own usually produces modest weight loss [9]; the guidelines find that activity combined with eating changes works best [4]. That is why your reps, sets, cardio steps and walking minutes are the main measures of progress here. For advice on eating, see a registered dietitian [11].

### Signs to slow down or seek advice

- Effort of 8/10 or more, form breaking down, or reps dropping: follow the hard-session rules in section 7.
- Soreness lasting more than 48 hours: remove one round for the rest of the week.
- You could not talk during cardio work intervals: repeat the step rather than moving on.
- Pain, joint discomfort, or any of the warning signs in section 2: stop and follow the advice there.

### After week 12

Keep going with the Phase 3 sessions, or start a new 4-8 week block, in line with the advice to change your routine every 4-8 weeks [13]. Keep building the optional walking toward 150-300 minutes of moderate activity a week [5][12]; more activity brings more benefit, and people should build up to it gradually [4].
