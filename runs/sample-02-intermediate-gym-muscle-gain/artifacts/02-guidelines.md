# Evidence-Based Guidelines

## Summary

The user is a 28-year-old intermediate lifter with about 2 years of consistent training. The goal is general
hypertrophy. They train in a full commercial gym on Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri for 60 minutes, strength-only (warm-up but no
dedicated cardio), with no reported limitations. The evidence points to the following:

- **Frequency:** 4 sessions/week is within the 3-4 days/week range ACSM gives for intermediate lifters
  ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  An upper/lower split lets each major muscle be trained twice a week. Twice-weekly training beat once-weekly in a
  meta-analysis ([Schoenfeld et al. 2016](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27102172&resultType=core&format=json)),
  but a newer meta-regression found frequency has little effect once weekly volume is matched
  ([Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Volume:** at least 10 sets per muscle per week
  ([ACSM 2026 Position Stand](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json)).
  More sets produce more growth, with diminishing returns
  ([Schoenfeld et al. 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27433992&resultType=core&format=json),
  [Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Load and effort:** use mainly 6-12 RM loads for hypertrophy
  ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  Muscle growth is similar across a wide range of loads
  ([Schoenfeld et al. 2017, loads](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:28834797&resultType=core&format=json)).
  Going to failure is not required
  ([Refalo et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:36334240&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Rest:** longer rests between sets (3 min rather than 1 min) gave more growth in trained men
  ([Schoenfeld et al. 2016, rest](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26605807&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Progression:** add 2-10% load once you can do 1-2 reps above the target
  ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  Use planned deloads, meaning periods of reduced training stress
  ([Bell et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10511399/fullTextXML)).
- **Recommended length:** 12 weeks in two 5-week progressive blocks, each followed by a deload week. This confirms
  the provisional length.

This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

## Recommended Program Length

**12 weeks** (provisional length confirmed). Structure:

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1: Build (accumulation) | 1-5 | Re-establish technique and baseline loads. Start at moderate volume (about 10-12 hard sets per major muscle per week) and stop 2-3 reps short of failure. Add load or reps each week. |
| Deload 1 | 6 | Cut sets by about a third to a half, keep loads moderate, stay further from failure. |
| Phase 2: Progress (higher volume and effort) | 7-11 | Increase volume step by step toward the upper end of the session budget (about 12-16 hard sets per major muscle per week). Bring effort to 1-2 reps short of failure, closer on isolation work. Keep progressing load on the main lifts. |
| Deload 2 and reassess | 12 | Reduced volume as in week 6. Retest working weights (for example, the reps done at a fixed load) to plan the next cycle. |

Justification:

- The weekly volume floor of 10 sets per muscle comes from the
  [ACSM 2026 Position Stand](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json).
  Raising volume across phases follows the dose-response evidence
  ([Schoenfeld et al. 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27433992&resultType=core&format=json),
  [Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)).
  The exact set ranges (10-12, then 12-16) are a programming judgment for a 4 x 60 min budget, not values stated in
  those sources.
- In the Delphi consensus on deloading, experts agreed on the following
  ([Bell et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10511399/fullTextXML)):
  - a deload can sit within each mesocycle or between mesocycles;
  - it can be pre-planned, autoregulated, or both;
  - it can cut sets, reps or training days, or reduce proximity to failure, while intensity can stay the same.

  The roughly 7-day length and the every-4-6-weeks rhythm come from the earlier literature cited in that paper's
  background, not from the panel's consensus. That is why the deload sits after a 5-week block.
- Periodization models had no consistent effect on outcomes in the
  [ACSM 2026 Position Stand](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json).
  So the phase structure is a simple way to organise progressive overload and fatigue, not a requirement in itself.
- Twelve weeks gives two full progressive blocks with recovery. That suits an intermediate lifter with no deadline,
  for whom gains come more slowly than in a beginner. ACSM's 2009 stand scales frequency and volume up with training
  status ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).

## Details

### Weekly volume and frequency

- **Public health baseline:** adults should do muscle-strengthening activity for all major muscle groups on at least
  2 days/week, plus 150 min of moderate (or 75 min of vigorous) aerobic activity
  ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html),
  [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/),
  [WHO 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)).
  The 4 strength sessions more than meet the strengthening guideline. Because the user chose no dedicated cardio,
  the program alone does not meet the aerobic guideline. Everyday activity such as walking can count toward it. It
  is noted here as information only and is not prescribed.
- **Training frequency:**
  - ACSM recommends 3-4 days/week for intermediate trainees
    ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Training each muscle at least twice a week gave more hypertrophy than once a week when volume was matched
    ([Schoenfeld et al. 2016](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27102172&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - *Disagreement:* a larger 2026 meta-regression (67 studies) found frequency effects on hypertrophy compatible
    with being negligible once weekly volume is accounted for
    ([Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Practical reading: an upper/lower split (each muscle twice a week) is a convenient way to fit enough weekly
    volume into 60-minute sessions. The exact frequency matters less than total volume.
- **Weekly volume:**
  - Use at least 10 sets per muscle per week
    ([ACSM 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json),
    [ACSM summary, March 2026](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)).
  - Each extra weekly set was linked to slightly more growth (about 0.37% per set)
    ([Schoenfeld et al. 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27433992&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Returns diminish as volume rises
    ([Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Multiple-set, higher-volume programs are recommended for maximising hypertrophy
    ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).

### Strength training dose

- **Load and reps:**
  - For hypertrophy, ACSM recommends loads of 1-12 RM, with emphasis on 6-12 RM
    ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Low-load (60% 1RM or less) and high-load training to failure gave similar hypertrophy. High loads gave more
    1RM strength
    ([Schoenfeld et al. 2017, loads](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:28834797&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Practical reading: main compound lifts at about 6-10 reps, accessories at about 10-15 reps.
- **Proximity to failure:**
  - Training to momentary failure was not better than stopping short of it, and the relationship may be non-linear
    ([Refalo et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:36334240&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - The ACSM 2026 overview likewise found no consistent effect of training to failure
    ([ACSM 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - Practical reading: stop most sets 1-3 reps short of failure. Keep heavy barbell compounds at 2 or more reps in
    reserve for safety. Isolation exercises can go closer.
- **Rest between sets:**
  - ACSM's 2009 stand suggests 1-2 min for hypertrophy
    ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json)).
  - *Disagreement:* in resistance-trained men, 3-min rests produced more strength and more thigh hypertrophy than
    1-min rests
    ([Schoenfeld et al. 2016, rest](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26605807&resultType=core&format=json)).
    This is the newer and more specific evidence for trained lifters.
  - Practical reading: about 2-3 min on compound lifts and about 1-2 min on accessories. This keeps sessions within
    60 minutes.
- **Exercise selection:**
  - Equipment type and exercise complexity had no consistent effect on outcomes
    ([ACSM 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC12965823&resultType=core&format=json)).
    So the user's preference for free weights, with cables and machines as accessories, is compatible with the goal.
  - Leaving out the leg extension does not limit results. Quadriceps volume can come from squat, lunge and press
    patterns.
  - Programs should be individualised to goals and enjoyment for long-term adherence
    ([ACSM summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)).

### Cardio dose (if applicable)

Not applicable. The modality is strength-only. A short general warm-up is part of each session (see Limitation-specific
safety guidance).

### Recovery

- **Between sessions:**
  - Give a muscle group about 48 hours before training it hard again
    ([Better Health Channel, 2022](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  - With an upper/lower split on Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri, each muscle group gets about 72 hours or more between sessions.
- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours per night
  ([CDC, 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)).
- **Deloads:** a deload is a period of reduced training stress to reduce physical and psychological fatigue
  ([Bell et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10511399/fullTextXML)).
  - Experts agreed that it can be done by any of these:
    - reducing sets, reps or training days;
    - reducing proximity to failure;
    - keeping intensity the same while volume drops.
  - Experts also agreed it can be planned in advance or autoregulated.
  - Weeks 6 and 12 are planned deloads. An extra lighter week is reasonable if performance drops for 2 or more
    sessions in a row.

### Progression and rate of change

- **Load progression (double progression):**
  - Work within a rep range.
  - When you can do 1-2 reps above the target range, raise the load by about 2-10%
    ([ACSM 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:19204579&resultType=core&format=json),
    [Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  - In kg, that usually means about +2.5-5 kg on lower-body barbell lifts and about +1-2.5 kg on upper-body lifts or
    per dumbbell. These figures are a unit conversion for practical plate increments.
- **Volume progression:**
  - Add sets gradually between phases, as in the program length table.
  - Volume increases growth with diminishing returns
    ([Pelland et al. 2026](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:41343037&resultType=core&format=json)),
    so adding sets when recovery and session time allow is reasonable.
- **Variation:** change the routine every 4-8 weeks to keep improving
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  This fits a moderate exercise swap between Phase 1 and Phase 2 while the main lifts stay the same.
- **Rate of change:**
  - No fetched source gave a safe or typical monthly rate of muscle gain, so no target is stated.
  - Track progress by loads and reps on the main lifts and by consistency.
  - No bodyweight or calorie targets are set (outside the scope of this program).

### Limitation-specific safety guidance

No limitations were reported. General safety guidance:

- **Warm-up:** about 5 minutes of light aerobic activity (for example, rowing or cycling), then dynamic movements
  through the full range of motion
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Then do lighter ramp-up sets of the first compound lift (programming practice).
- **Technique first:** keep form good as the load rises
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  Use safety pins or a spotter on squat and bench (practical gym safety).
- **Effort on heavy compounds:** keep 2 or more reps in reserve on barbell squats, deadlifts and presses. Failure is
  not needed for growth
  ([Refalo et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:36334240&resultType=core&format=json)).
- **Build gradually:** NHLBI advises building activity into your routine slowly and safely
  ([NHLBI, 2022](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/physical-activity/risks)).

### Red flags to stop and seek care

- **Stop exercising** if you have chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness. Seek urgent medical advice
  if these do not ease quickly with rest
  ([British Heart Foundation, 2024](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  That article is written for people with heart conditions, but the same stop signs apply generally.
- **Talk to a doctor** about chest pain or dizziness during physical activity
  ([NHLBI, 2022](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/physical-activity/risks)).
- **Pain:** if you feel pain or discomfort, stop progressing and consult a health professional
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
