# Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery Plan

## Summary

- **Schedule it fits:** 4 strength sessions a week from the training plan: Upper A (Monday), Lower A (Tuesday),
  Upper B (Thursday) and Lower B (Friday). Each one is capped at 60 minutes, and that includes the 10-minute warm-up.
  Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday are rest days. There is no cardio stage (strength-only), so the only aerobic work
  prescribed is the 5 easy minutes inside each warm-up.
- **Warm-up:** this is the training plan's 10-minute warm-up, filled in for each session type: 5 min easy rower or
  bike, 2 min dynamic drills for that day's lifts, then about 3 min of ramp-up sets of the first main lift. It is never
  cut.
- **Cool-down:** it uses only the time left under 60 minutes. That is 3 minutes on the full sessions (estimated
  54-57 min), 5-8 minutes on Phase 1 Upper A (about 50 min), and a full 5-8 minute routine in the deload weeks
  (35-45 min). A longer stretch is optional on rest days and never added to a training day.
- **Rest days:** Wednesday sits between the lower and upper days. Saturday and Sunday follow Lower B. Each muscle group
  gets 72 hours or more between sessions. Everyday walking is fine, and an optional 6-8 minute mobility routine is
  suggested on Wednesday and one weekend day. This is not a training session.
- **Deloads and missed sessions:** these are the training plan's rules, unchanged. Weeks 6 and 12 are planned deloads,
  and an extra lighter week is taken if performance drops across lifts for 2 or more sessions or sleep or soreness gets
  clearly worse. Never double up, and repeat the last week at about 90% after a week or more off.
- **Habits:** 7 or more hours of sleep with regular bed and wake times, a short sleep and soreness note in the training
  log, and no diet, calorie or supplement advice. A registered (accredited sports) dietitian is the right source for
  nutrition.
- **Limitations:** none reported. Preventive care targets the joints loaded hardest by heavy free-weight compounds
  (lower back, shoulders, knees).
- **Red flags:** stop and seek professional advice for chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, sharp pain,
  swelling, numbness or tingling, and the other signs listed below.

This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

## Details

### Warm-up

Warm up before strength training with about five minutes of light cardio such as walking, cycling or rowing, plus a
few moving stretches, meaning "slow controlled movements through the full range of motion"
([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
Start each session at a slower pace so the body can prepare
([BHF](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
The structure and its 10 minutes come from the training plan. This section only fills in the drills and doses for each
session.

**Part 1: light aerobic activity, 5 min (every session).** Easy rowing machine or stationary bike at a pace where you
can talk in full sentences. On lower days the bike is a good choice because it warms the knees and hips. On upper days
the rower also moves the shoulders and upper back. This is warm-up only, not a cardio session.

**Part 2: dynamic range-of-motion drills, 2 min.** Use the drills the training plan names for each day type. Move
slowly through a comfortable range, with no bouncing.

| Session | Drill and dose (about 2 min in total) | Why it fits that session |
|---|---|---|
| Upper A (Mon): bench, row, overhead press | Arm circles 10 forward and 10 back; shoulder rolls 10 back; thoracic rotations (half-kneeling or side-lying) 5 per side | Opens the shoulders for bench and overhead press, and the upper back for the bent-over row |
| Lower A (Tue): back squat, RDL, split squat | Leg swings front-to-back and side-to-side, 8 per direction per leg (hand on a rack for balance); hip circles 5 each way; ankle rocks (knee over the toes, heel down) 8 per side | Hip and ankle range for squat depth and the split squat's front knee |
| Upper B (Thu): incline press, pull-up, dip | Arm circles 10 forward and 10 back; shoulder rolls 10 back; thoracic rotations 5 per side; ending with a 10-15 s dead hang from the pull-up bar to set the grip | Overhead and pressing range for pull-ups, incline press and dips |
| Lower B (Fri): deadlift, leg press, hip thrust | Leg swings 8 per direction per leg; hip circles 5 each way; ankle rocks 6 per side; ending with 8 bodyweight hip hinges (hands on the hips, push them back, flat back) | Rehearses the deadlift hinge and warms the hips and hamstrings |

**Part 3: ramp-up sets of the first main lift, about 3 min.** This is the training plan's rule: 2-3 short sets at
about 40%, 60% and 80% of the working load, 3-5 reps each, with short rests.

| Session | First main lift | Ramp-up note |
|---|---|---|
| Upper A | Barbell bench press | Set the safety pins during the first set. Use the 80% set to check the bar path. |
| Lower A | Barbell back squat | Set the safety pins first. Use the 40% set to check depth and knee tracking. |
| Upper B | Barbell incline press (Phase 1) / dumbbell incline press (Phase 2) | For dumbbells, use 2 lighter pairs (about 50% and 75%) instead of 3 steps. |
| Lower B | Barbell deadlift | Reset each rep exactly as in the working sets. Brace on every rep, including at 40%. |

The second compound in a pair (bent-over row, lat pulldown, pull-up, RDL) is warmed by the first round. Its first
working set can be a "feeler" at the lower end of its rep range. It does not need extra ramp-up sets, so the session
times in the training plan stay as they are.

**Rules.** Do not skip the warm-up, even when short on time. The training plan says to drop the last round of the final
pair or tri-set instead. In the deload weeks (6 and 12) the warm-up stays the same. In week 12 the ramp-up sets lead
into the retest set.

### Cool-down and mobility

Finish each session at a slower pace so the body can recover
([BHF](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
The cool-down uses only the minutes left under the 60-minute cap. It is never added on top of a full session.

**Cool-down time per session** (estimated session times are from the training plan):

| Weeks | Upper A (Mon) | Lower A (Tue) | Upper B (Thu) | Lower B (Fri) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-5 (Phase 1) | about 50 min, so the 6-8 min full upper routine | about 57 min, so the 3-min lower routine | about 56 min, so the 3-min upper routine | about 56 min, so the 3-min lower routine |
| 6 (deload) | 35-40 min, so the 6-8 min full upper routine | 35-40 min, so the 6-8 min full lower routine | 35-40 min, so the 6-8 min full upper routine | 35-40 min, so the 6-8 min full lower routine |
| 7-11 (Phase 2) | about 57 min, so the 3-min upper routine | about 57 min, so the 3-min lower routine | about 57 min, so the 3-min upper routine | about 54 min, so the 3-min lower routine (5 min if the optional deadlift back-off set is not done) |
| 12 (deload and retest) | 35-45 min, so the 6-8 min full upper routine | 35-45 min, so the 6-8 min full lower routine | 35-45 min, so the 6-8 min full upper routine | 35-45 min, so the 6-8 min full lower routine |

If a session runs long, the cool-down shrinks first (to at least 1 minute of easy walking and slow breathing). Then the
training plan's rule applies: drop the last round of the final pair or tri-set.

**Stretch technique.** Hold each stretch still at mild tension, with no bouncing, and breathe normally. Stop if you feel
pain, or tingling in the arms or legs
([RJAH NHS chest stretch](https://ourspace.rjah.nhs.uk/exercise/the-chest-stretch/)).

**3-minute upper routine (Mon, Thu).**

| Move | Dose | How |
|---|---|---|
| Easy walk around the gym, slow breathing | 30 s | Shoulders relaxed, let the heart rate settle |
| Chest stretch | 20 s x 3 | Sit forward on a bench. Thumbs point up, open the arms out to the side until you feel the front of the chest stretch, shoulders back and down, draw the shoulder blades together ([RJAH NHS](https://ourspace.rjah.nhs.uk/exercise/the-chest-stretch/)) |
| Neck stretch | 5 s x 3 per side | Seated, hold one shoulder down with the opposite hand and tilt the head away ([NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)) |
| Sideways bend | 2 s x 3 per side | Standing, slide one hand down the side of the leg as far as is comfortable, which stretches the side of the trunk and lats ([NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)) |

**6-8 minute full upper routine (Phase 1 Mon, deload weeks).** Do the 3-minute upper routine, then add:

- Neck rotation: 5 s x 3 per side ([NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)).
- Buttock and hamstring stretches from the NHS after-exercise routine: 15-20 s per side
  ([NHS stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)). Upper days
  still load the hips and hamstrings in the bent-over row hinge.

**3-minute lower routine (Tue, Fri).** All moves are from the NHS after-exercise routine, with 15-20 s holds
([NHS stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)).

| Move | Dose | How |
|---|---|---|
| Easy walk, slow breathing | 30 s | Let the heart rate settle after the last round |
| Calf stretch | 15-20 s per side | Step one leg forward with the knee bent. Keep the back leg straight and lower its heel. Use a wall or rack for support |
| Hamstring stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, one foot on the floor. Hold the other straight leg below the knee and draw it gently toward you |
| Thigh (quad) stretch | 15-20 s per side | Lying on your side, hold the top foot and draw the heel toward the buttock, knees together |

**6-8 minute full lower routine (deload weeks).** Do the 3-minute lower routine, then add the buttock stretch (15-20 s
per side) and the inner thigh stretch (15-20 s, soles of the feet together, knees lowered gently). This completes the
NHS 5-minute routine
([NHS stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)). Spend any
remaining minute on whichever area feels tightest.

**Rest-day mobility (optional, about 6-8 minutes, Wednesday and one weekend day).** NHS flexibility exercises are
recommended at least twice a week. Start gently and build up slowly
([NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)).

| Move | Dose | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Neck rotation | 5 s x 3 per side | Upper back and neck after pressing and pulling days |
| Chest stretch | 20 s x 3 | Front of the shoulders after bench, incline and dips ([RJAH NHS](https://ourspace.rjah.nhs.uk/exercise/the-chest-stretch/)) |
| Sideways bend | 2 s x 3 per side | Trunk and lats after rows and pull-ups |
| Calf stretch at the wall | 3 per side | Calves are trained on both lower days |
| Buttock, hamstring and thigh stretches | 15-20 s per side | Hips and legs after squats, deadlifts and split squats |

This routine is light and does not count as a training session. Skipping it never counts as a missed session. Do not
add any loaded training on rest days.

### Rest days, deloads and missed sessions

**Rest-day placement.** Rest each muscle group for at least 48 hours before working it again
([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
The training plan's upper/lower layout gives more than that.

| Day | Plan | Hours since the same muscles were last trained | Recovery focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Upper A | about 96 h since Upper B (Thu) | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Tuesday | Lower A | about 96 h since Lower B (Fri) | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Wednesday | Rest | - | Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility |
| Thursday | Upper B | about 72 h since Upper A | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Friday | Lower B | about 72 h since Lower A | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Saturday | Rest | - | Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility (here or Sunday) |
| Sunday | Rest | - | Check the training log (loads, reps, sleep, soreness) and plan the coming week |

Do not move two sessions for the same half of the body (upper or lower) onto back-to-back days.

**Planned deloads (as in the training plan).**

- **Week 6 (Deload 1):** the same Phase 1 sessions with every exercise cut to 2 sets (2-set exercises to 1). Main lifts
  at about 90% of the week-5 load, 4 or more reps in reserve, nothing near failure. Sessions take 35-40 min, so use the
  full cool-down routines and do the rest-day mobility on both suggested days. This follows the deload consensus: fewer
  sets and further from failure
  ([Bell et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10511399/fullTextXML)).
- **Week 12 (Deload 2 and retest):** the same reduction on the Phase 2 sessions. The first working set of each main
  lift is a retest set (week-7 load, as many reps as possible, stopping at 2 reps in reserve). Keep the full warm-up
  and ramp-up before every retest set.

**Autoregulated extra deload (from the training plan).** If performance falls on several lifts for 2 or more sessions
in a row, or sleep or soreness is clearly worse, take an extra lighter week run like week 6. Then continue where you
stopped. Deloads may be planned or autoregulated
([Bell et al. 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10511399/fullTextXML)). The sleep and soreness
note described below makes this trigger easy to spot.

**Hard sets and stalled lifts (from the training plan).** After a hard set (below the rep range, or 0-1 reps in reserve
on a barbell compound), finish at -5% and keep that load next session. If a lift misses the bottom of its range 2
sessions in a row, cut its load by about 10% and build back up.

**Missed sessions (from the training plan).**

- **One missed session:** do not double up. Do the missed session on the next training day and push the rest of the
  week back by one session. Keep the planned loads. If a session is left over at the end of the week, skip it and start
  the next week with Upper A. When a session moves, check the rest-day table: an upper session and a lower session on
  back-to-back days is fine, but two upper or two lower sessions on back-to-back days are not.
- **One week or more missed:** repeat the last completed week at about 90% of its loads, then resume progression. Keep
  the full warm-up.
- **Short on time:** keep the full 10-minute warm-up and the main lift. Drop the last round of the final pair or
  tri-set and reduce the cool-down to 1 minute of easy walking.
- **Feeling unwell (for example a fever or a heavy cold):** skip the session and treat it as missed under the rules
  above.

### Sleep and recovery habits

- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours a night
  ([CDC About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). Habits that help
  ([CDC About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)):
  - go to bed and get up at the same time every day, including weekends after Friday's session;
  - keep the bedroom quiet, relaxing and cool;
  - turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime;
  - avoid large meals and alcohol before bedtime, and caffeine in the afternoon or evening. This matters if you use
    caffeinated drinks before late-day sessions.
- **Training log:** next to loads and reps, note hours of sleep and a 0-10 soreness rating for each session. Two or
  more sessions with clearly worse sleep or soreness, together with falling performance, is the training plan's cue
  for an extra lighter week.
- **Everyday movement:** walking on rest days is fine and is not prescribed as cardio. The aerobic guideline is
  covered as information only in the guidelines research. It is not part of this plan, because the user chose no
  dedicated cardio.
- **Comfort:** wear loose, comfortable clothing for mobility work and keep water nearby
  ([NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/)).
- **Nutrition:** this plan gives no diet plans, calorie targets or supplement advice. Food matters for muscle gain,
  and a registered or accredited sports dietitian is the right source. Supplements should not be taken without the
  advice of a medical practitioner or an accredited sports dietitian
  ([Better Health Channel, sporting performance and food](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/sporting-performance-and-food)).

### Limitation-specific care

No injuries, medical conditions or current pain were reported. The care below is preventive. It targets the joints
loaded hardest by this program's heavy free-weight compounds, and it uses only drills and stretches already listed
above.

- **Lower back (deadlift, Romanian deadlift, bent-over row, back squat).**
  - Rehearse the hinge in the Friday warm-up (bodyweight hip hinges). Brace on every ramp-up rep.
  - Keep 2 or more reps in reserve on heavy barbell compounds, as the training plan requires.
  - Pay attention to safety and form to reduce the risk of injury
    ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
  - Hamstring and buttock stretches in the cool-down and on rest days help with hinge range.
- **Shoulders (bench, incline, overhead press, chest dip, pull-up).**
  - Use the arm circles, shoulder rolls and thoracic rotations before every upper session. Do the dead hang before
    pull-ups.
  - Use the chest stretch afterwards. Stop at once if it causes pain or tingling in the arms
    ([RJAH NHS](https://ourspace.rjah.nhs.uk/exercise/the-chest-stretch/)).
  - If the front of the shoulder hurts on dips, use the training plan's "Easier" option (close-grip bench press).
- **Knees and ankles (back squat, Bulgarian split squat, leg press).**
  - Do ankle rocks and leg swings before lower sessions.
  - Use the 40% squat ramp-up set to check knee tracking.
  - Calf and thigh stretches go in the cool-down.
  - If a knee feels painful rather than just worked, switch to the "Easier" option (goblet squat, rear lunge, shorter
    leg-press range).
- **General.**
  - If you feel discomfort or pain, contact a health professional before progressing the program
    ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
    Until then, follow the training plan's pain rule: stop progressing that exercise and use its "Easier" option.
  - If an injury or condition appears, some stretches may need changing. Ask a physiotherapist or health care
    professional which ones are right for you
    ([NHS stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising)).

### Red flags

**Stop and seek professional advice** if any of these happen during or after a session or mobility work:

- **Chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness:** stop exercising. Seek urgent medical
  advice if the symptoms do not ease quickly with rest
  ([BHF](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/activity/finding-safe-exercise-limits)).
  Talk to a doctor about chest pain or dizziness during physical activity
  ([NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/physical-activity/risks)).
- **Sharp or sudden pain in a joint or muscle, or pain that is more than normal muscle effort:** stop that exercise and
  contact a health professional before progressing
  ([Better Health Channel](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/resistance-training-health-benefits)).
- **Swelling or bruising, weakness, or difficulty putting weight on a limb or using it normally after a twist or
  strain:** stop and rest the area. These are typical signs of a sprain or strain
  ([NHS sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)).
  - Get urgent advice (NHS 111 or an urgent treatment centre, or your local equivalent) if the injury is very painful
    or getting worse, you cannot put weight on it or walk more than a few steps, swelling or bruising is large or
    worsening, or the area is very stiff.
  - Call emergency services or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the body part has changed shape, the area is numb or
    tingling, or the skin looks blue or grey or feels cold
    ([NHS sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)).
- **Numbness, tingling or pins and needles in the arms or legs during lifting or stretching:** stop the movement
  ([RJAH NHS](https://ourspace.rjah.nhs.uk/exercise/the-chest-stretch/)) and seek professional advice if it does not
  settle quickly.
- **Pain that is getting worse, or not better after a few days of self-care:** see a GP
  ([NHS sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)). Do not progress the affected
  exercise until you have been advised.
