# 12-Week Gym Muscle-Gain Program for Intermediate Lifters

For an intermediate lifter (about 2 years of consistent training) building muscle in a full commercial gym: 4 upper/lower strength sessions a week (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday), 60 minutes each including the warm-up.

## 1. Overview

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Goal | Muscle gain (general hypertrophy), no specific target or deadline |
| Program length | 12 weeks: Build (weeks 1-5), lighter deload week (week 6), Progress (weeks 7-11), deload and retest (week 12) |
| Sessions per week | 4 strength sessions (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday), no separate cardio sessions |
| Minutes per session | 60 or less, including a 10-minute warm-up (estimated 50-57 minutes in normal weeks, 35-45 minutes in deload weeks) |
| Equipment | Full commercial gym: barbells, racks, dumbbells, cables, machines, pull-up bar, dip station, benches, and a rower or bike for the warm-up |
| Units | Metric (kg) |

The program uses an upper/lower split: Upper A (Monday), Lower A (Tuesday), Upper B (Thursday), Lower B (Friday). Every major muscle is trained twice a week, which is a convenient way to fit enough weekly work into 60-minute sessions [5]. Total weekly volume matters more than the exact frequency [6]. Each session is built around a free-weight compound lift (bench press, back squat, incline press and pull-up, deadlift), as you prefer, with cables and machines used only for accessories. The leg extension machine is not used; your quadriceps get their work from squats, split squats and the leg press [1].

Main lifts use 6-10 reps and accessories 8-20 reps, mostly within the 6-12 rep range recommended for hypertrophy [3]; muscle growth is similar across a wide range of loads [7]. You stop most sets a few reps short of failure, because going to failure is not needed for growth [8]. Large muscle groups start at about 10-12 hard sets a week, at or above the recommended minimum of 10 [1][2], and rise to about 12-14 (up to about 16 for glutes) in Phase 2, because more sets bring more growth with diminishing returns [4][6].

Why 12 weeks: it gives two full progressive blocks of 5 weeks, each followed by a lighter deload week. That suits an intermediate lifter with no deadline, for whom gains come more slowly than for a beginner [3]. A deload is a planned period of reduced training stress to shed fatigue [10]. The phase structure is simply a way to organise progress and fatigue; no single periodization model has been shown to be better [1].

The 4 strength sessions more than meet the public-health guideline of muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days a week [11][12][13]. Because you chose no dedicated cardio, the program on its own does not meet the separate aerobic guideline of 150 minutes of moderate (or 75 minutes of vigorous) activity a week [11][12][13]. Everyday activity such as walking can count toward it. This is information only; no cardio is prescribed.

## 2. Safety Notice

This program is general exercise information, not medical advice. You reported no injuries, no medical conditions, no current pain and no restrictions from a doctor, so no medical adaptations were needed. Your 2 years of consistent training with the main barbell lifts make standard intermediate loading appropriate. The program still builds in these safety habits:

- **Never skip the warm-up.** Every session starts with 10 minutes of light aerobic activity, moving stretches and lighter ramp-up sets of the first lift [14]. Start and finish each session at a slower pace [16].
- **Technique first.** Keep your form good as the load rises [14]. Set the safety pins on the squat, bench and incline press (or use a spotter).
- **Stay away from failure on heavy barbell lifts.** Keep at least 2 reps in reserve (2 more good reps you could still do) on barbell squats, deadlifts and presses in every phase [8].
- **Build gradually** [17]. Loads go up only when you have earned it with reps (section 7).
- **Preventive care** for the joints that heavy free-weight lifts load most (lower back, shoulders, knees) is in section 8.
- **Nutrition:** this program contains no diet plans, calorie targets or supplement advice. Food matters for muscle gain, and a registered or accredited sports dietitian is the right source. Do not take supplements without the advice of a medical practitioner or an accredited sports dietitian [21].

**Stop and seek professional advice** if any of these happen during or after a session or mobility work:

- **Chest pain or discomfort, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness:** stop exercising. Seek urgent medical advice if the symptoms do not ease quickly with rest [16]. Talk to a doctor about chest pain or dizziness during physical activity [17].
- **Sharp or sudden pain in a joint or muscle, or pain that is more than normal muscle effort:** stop that exercise and contact a health professional before progressing it [14].
- **Swelling or bruising, weakness, or difficulty putting weight on a limb or using it normally after a twist or strain:** stop and rest the area. These are typical signs of a sprain or strain [22].
  - Get urgent advice (NHS 111 or an urgent treatment centre, or your local equivalent) if the injury is very painful or getting worse, you cannot put weight on it or walk more than a few steps, swelling or bruising is large or worsening, or the area is very stiff.
  - Call emergency services or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the body part has changed shape, the area is numb or tingling, or the skin looks blue or grey or feels cold [22].
- **Numbness, tingling or pins and needles in the arms or legs during lifting or stretching:** stop the movement [20] and seek professional advice if it does not settle quickly.
- **Pain that is getting worse, or not better after a few days of self-care:** see a GP [22]. Do not progress the affected exercise until you have been advised.
- **If an injury or condition appears,** some stretches may need changing. Ask a physiotherapist or health care professional which ones are right for you [18].
- **Feeling unwell** (for example a fever or a heavy cold): skip the session and treat it as missed (section 7).

## 4. Program at a Glance

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Intensity guidance | Key changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1: Build | 1-5 | Re-establish technique and baseline loads; add load or reps each week | 2-3 reps in reserve on every set. About 10-12 hard sets per major muscle per week | Main lifts 6-8 or 6-10 reps, accessories 8-15 reps. Week 1 is a calibration week |
| Deload 1 | 6 | Shed fatigue before Phase 2 | 4 or more reps in reserve, nothing near failure. About 5-6 sets per muscle per week | Same Phase 1 sessions with fewer sets; main lifts at about 90% of the week-5 load. Sessions about 35-40 min |
| Phase 2: Progress | 7-11 | More volume and effort; keep adding load on the main lifts | 2 reps in reserve on barbell compounds; 1-2 on dumbbell, bodyweight and machine compounds; 0-1 on the last set of isolation work. About 12-14 sets per major muscle per week | Extra sets on the main lifts, calves and leg curls. Incline press switches to dumbbells. New accessories (rear lateral raise, hammer curl). Accessory reps 8-20 |
| Deload 2 and retest | 12 | Recover and measure progress | 4 or more reps in reserve, except one retest set per main lift at 2 reps in reserve. About 6-7 sets per muscle per week | Phase 2 sessions with fewer sets, plus a retest set that sets your starting loads for the next cycle. Sessions about 35-45 min |

**Reps in reserve (RIR)** means how many more good reps you could still do when you stop a set. "2 RIR" means you stop with about 2 good reps left. Failure is not needed for growth [8], and the deload approach (fewer sets, further from failure) follows expert consensus [10].

Large muscle groups meet the 10-set minimum in Phase 1 and rise in Phase 2 [1][4][6]. Smaller muscles (delts, arms, calves, abs) get 6-8 direct sets plus their compound work, a choice made to fit 4 x 60 minutes. Pushing and pulling are balanced: 12 compound sets each in Phase 1 and 14 each in Phase 2, plus rear-delt work.

## 5. Weekly Schedule

The weekly layout is the same in all 12 weeks:

| Day | Session | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Strength: Upper A (horizontal press and row focus), then cool-down | up to 60 min |
| Tuesday | Strength: Lower A (squat focus), then cool-down | up to 60 min |
| Wednesday | Rest. Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility routine | optional 6-8 min |
| Thursday | Strength: Upper B (incline press and vertical pull focus), then cool-down | up to 60 min |
| Friday | Strength: Lower B (hinge focus), then cool-down | up to 60 min |
| Saturday | Rest. Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility routine (here or Sunday) | optional 6-8 min |
| Sunday | Rest. Check your training log (loads, reps, sleep, soreness) and plan the coming week | - |

The upper days are 72 hours apart (Monday to Thursday), and so are the lower days (Tuesday to Friday). From Thursday to Monday and from Friday to Tuesday there are about 96 hours. All of this is more than the roughly 48 hours of rest per muscle group that is recommended [14]. There are no cardio sessions; the only aerobic work is the 5 easy minutes inside each warm-up.

**Inside each session:** a 10-minute warm-up (section 8), the workout (section 6), then a cool-down that uses only the minutes left under 60. The cool-down is never added on top of a full session. Session length and cool-down change by phase:

| Weeks | Upper A (Mon) | Lower A (Tue) | Upper B (Thu) | Lower B (Fri) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-5 (Phase 1) | about 50 min + 6-8 min full upper cool-down | about 57 min + 3-min lower cool-down | about 56 min + 3-min upper cool-down | about 56 min + 3-min lower cool-down |
| 6 (deload) | 35-40 min + 6-8 min full upper cool-down | 35-40 min + 6-8 min full lower cool-down | 35-40 min + 6-8 min full upper cool-down | 35-40 min + 6-8 min full lower cool-down |
| 7-11 (Phase 2) | about 57 min + 3-min upper cool-down | about 57 min + 3-min lower cool-down | about 57 min + 3-min upper cool-down | about 54 min + 3-min lower cool-down (5 min if you skip the optional deadlift back-off set) |
| 12 (deload and retest) | 35-45 min + 6-8 min full upper cool-down | 35-45 min + 6-8 min full lower cool-down | 35-45 min + 6-8 min full upper cool-down | 35-45 min + 6-8 min full lower cool-down |

**The 60-minute limit always wins.** If a session runs long, shrink the cool-down first (to at least 1 minute of easy walking and slow breathing), then drop the last round of the final pair or tri-set. Never cut the warm-up or the main lift.

**Moving sessions:** an upper and a lower session on back-to-back days is fine, but never put two upper or two lower sessions on back-to-back days.

## 6. Workouts

**How to read the tables.**

- Every session starts with the 10-minute warm-up in section 8.
- Exercises marked A1/A2 (or B1/B2) are **paired sets**, and C1/C2/C3 are **tri-sets**. Do them in alternating rounds: one set of A1, the listed rest, one set of A2, the listed rest, then repeat. Paired exercises never compete for the same main muscles (for example a press with a pull, or a squat with calves), so each muscle still gets about 3-3.75 minutes between its own sets on the paired main lifts. Longer rests like this gave more growth in trained lifters [9], and pairing keeps the session within 60 minutes.
- Exercises with a plain number (1, 2, 3) are done on their own: all sets, with the listed rest between them.
- "Per leg" means do all reps on one leg, then the other.
- Numbers in brackets after an exercise name are exercise dataset ids (see section 10).

### Deload weeks (week 6 and week 12)

## 7. Progression Rules

### Double progression (all phases)

Load steps follow the guideline of adding about 2-10% once you can do 1-2 reps more than the target [3][14]. Each rep range works as a target (the bottom of the range) plus room to grow.

1. Start each exercise at a load that gives the bottom of the rep range at the phase's reps-in-reserve target.
2. Each session, try to add 1 rep to at least one set at the same load, keeping the reps-in-reserve target.
3. When every set reaches the top of the range at the target reps in reserve, increase the load at the next session of that exercise:
   - back squat, deadlift, Romanian deadlift, glute bridge / hip thrust: +2.5-5 kg
   - bench press, incline bench, overhead press, bent-over row, skull crusher, barbell curl: +1-2.5 kg
   - dumbbell exercises: the next dumbbell size (+1-2.5 kg per dumbbell)
   - cable and machine exercises: the next pin (smallest step)
   - pull-up and chest dip: once all sets reach the top of the range with bodyweight, add 2.5 kg with a belt
4. After a load increase your reps drop back toward the bottom of the range, and you build them up again.

### Phase 1: Build (weeks 1-5)

- **Week 1 is calibration.** Choose loads that leave about 3 reps in reserve at the bottom of the range. If a set ends with 4 or more reps in reserve, add load next session even if you did not reach the top of the range.
- **Weeks 2-5:** add reps or load every week on each exercise. Keep 2-3 reps in reserve on every set.
- **Pass test (end of week 5):** each main lift (bench press, row, overhead press, squat, deadlift, incline bench, pull-up) has at least one load increase, or at least 4 more total reps than in week 1 at the same load. If a lift fails this test, start it in Phase 2 at the week-5 load rather than higher.

### Phase 2: Progress (weeks 7-11)

- **Week 7 loads:**
  - Main lifts carried over from Phase 1 (bench press, row, overhead press, squat, Romanian deadlift, deadlift, pull-up, chest dip): use the week-5 working load. After the deload it will feel lighter at the new effort target.
  - New or swapped exercises (dumbbell incline press, dumbbell rear lateral raise, dumbbell hammer curl, and exercises that moved to a different slot): pick a load that gives the bottom of the range at 2 reps in reserve.
- **Volume** is already higher through the extra sets in the Phase 2 tables (main lifts 3 to 4 sets; calves and leg curls 3 to 4).
- **Effort targets:** barbell compounds at 2 reps in reserve; dumbbell, bodyweight and machine compounds at 1-2; isolation work with the last set at 0-1.
- Keep using the double-progression load steps. Expect weekly progress mostly on accessories and on reps, and a load step on the main lifts every 1-2 weeks.
- **Optional extra volume:** only if the session is finishing under 55 minutes and your last two sessions went well, add one back-off set (8-10 reps at -10%) to the Friday deadlift in weeks 9-11.

### Deloads

- Weeks 6 and 12 are planned deloads (section 6, Deload weeks) [10].
- **Extra lighter week:** if performance falls on several lifts for 2 or more sessions in a row, or your sleep or soreness is clearly worse, take an extra lighter week run like week 6, then continue where you stopped. Deloads can be planned or taken when needed [10].

### Hard sets and stalled lifts

- **Hard set:** if a set falls below the bottom of the rep range, or a barbell compound reaches 0-1 reps in reserve, finish the remaining sets at -5% load. Keep that load next session.
- **Stalled lift:** if the same lift misses the bottom of its range in 2 sessions in a row, reduce its load by about 10% and build back up with double progression.
- **Pain:** if you feel pain (rather than normal muscle effort), stop progressing that exercise and use its "Easier" option. If the pain persists, see a health professional [14]. Stop exercising at once for chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or light-headedness [16][17] (section 2).

### Missed sessions

- **One missed session:** do not double up. Do the missed session on the next training day and push the rest of the week back by one session. Keep the planned loads. If a week ends with a session left over, skip it and start the next week with Upper A. Never put two upper or two lower sessions on back-to-back days.
- **One week or more missed:** repeat the last completed week at about 90% of its loads, then resume progression. Keep the full warm-up.
- **Short on time:** keep the full 10-minute warm-up and the main lift. Drop the last round of the final pair or tri-set and reduce the cool-down to 1 minute of easy walking.
- **Feeling unwell** (for example a fever or a heavy cold): skip the session and treat it as missed.

## 8. Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery

### Warm-up (every session, 10 minutes, inside the 60 minutes)

Warm up with about five minutes of light aerobic activity plus moving stretches, meaning slow, controlled movements through the full range of motion [14]. Starting at a slower pace lets your body prepare [16]. Never skip the warm-up; in deload weeks it stays the same.

**Part 1: light aerobic activity, 5 minutes.** Easy rowing machine or stationary bike at a pace where you can talk in full sentences. On lower days the bike is a good choice because it warms the knees and hips; on upper days the rower also moves the shoulders and upper back. This is a warm-up, not a cardio session.

**Part 2: moving stretches, about 2 minutes.** Move slowly through a comfortable range, with no bouncing.

| Session | Drills and dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Upper A (Mon) | Arm circles 10 forward and 10 back; shoulder rolls 10 back; thoracic rotations (half-kneeling or side-lying) 5 per side | Opens the shoulders for bench and overhead press, and the upper back for the bent-over row |
| Lower A (Tue) | Leg swings front-to-back and side-to-side, 8 per direction per leg (hand on a rack for balance); hip circles 5 each way; ankle rocks (knee over the toes, heel down) 8 per side | Hip and ankle range for squat depth and the split squat's front knee |
| Upper B (Thu) | Arm circles 10 forward and 10 back; shoulder rolls 10 back; thoracic rotations 5 per side; finish with a 10-15 s dead hang from the pull-up bar | Overhead and pressing range for pull-ups, incline press and dips |
| Lower B (Fri) | Leg swings 8 per direction per leg; hip circles 5 each way; ankle rocks 6 per side; finish with 8 bodyweight hip hinges (hands on the hips, push them back, flat back) | Rehearses the deadlift hinge and warms the hips and hamstrings |

**Part 3: ramp-up sets of the first main lift, about 3 minutes.** Do 2-3 short sets at about 40%, 60% and 80% of your working load, 3-5 reps each, with short rests.

| Session | First main lift | Ramp-up note |
|---|---|---|
| Upper A | Barbell bench press | Set the safety pins during the first set. Use the 80% set to check the bar path |
| Lower A | Barbell back squat | Set the safety pins first. Use the 40% set to check depth and knee tracking |
| Upper B | Barbell incline press (Phase 1) / dumbbell incline press (Phase 2) | With dumbbells, use 2 lighter pairs (about 50% and 75%) instead of 3 steps |
| Lower B | Barbell deadlift | Reset each rep exactly as in the working sets. Brace on every rep, including at 40% |

The second lift in a pair (bent-over row, lat pulldown, pull-up) and the Romanian deadlift that follows the squat are warmed up by the earlier work. Their first working set can be a "feeler" set at the lower end of the rep range; no extra ramp-up sets are needed. In week 12 the ramp-up sets lead into the retest set.

### Cool-down

Finish each session at a slower pace so your body can recover [16]. The cool-down only uses the time left under 60 minutes (see the table in section 5). Hold each stretch still at mild tension, with no bouncing, and breathe normally. Stop if you feel pain, or tingling in the arms or legs [20].

**3-minute upper routine (Monday, Thursday)**

| Move | Dose | How |
|---|---|---|
| Easy walk around the gym, slow breathing | 30 s | Shoulders relaxed, let your heart rate settle |
| Chest stretch | 20 s x 3 | Sit forward on a bench. Thumbs point up, open the arms out to the side until you feel the front of the chest stretch, shoulders back and down, shoulder blades drawn together [20] |
| Neck stretch | 5 s x 3 per side | Seated, hold one shoulder down with the opposite hand and tilt the head away [19] |
| Sideways bend | 2 s x 3 per side | Standing, slide one hand down the side of the leg as far as is comfortable, stretching the side of the trunk and the lats [19] |

**6-8 minute full upper routine (Phase 1 Monday and deload weeks).** Do the 3-minute upper routine, then add:

- Neck rotation: 5 s x 3 per side [19].
- Buttock and hamstring stretches: 15-20 s per side [18]. Upper days still load the hips and hamstrings in the bent-over row hinge.

**3-minute lower routine (Tuesday, Friday).** Holds of 15-20 seconds [18].

| Move | Dose | How |
|---|---|---|
| Easy walk, slow breathing | 30 s | Let your heart rate settle after the last round |
| Calf stretch | 15-20 s per side | Step one leg forward with the knee bent. Keep the back leg straight and lower its heel. Use a wall or rack for support |
| Hamstring stretch | 15-20 s per side | On your back, one foot on the floor. Hold the other straight leg below the knee and draw it gently toward you |
| Thigh (quad) stretch | 15-20 s per side | Lying on your side, hold the top foot and draw the heel toward the buttock, knees together |

**6-8 minute full lower routine (deload weeks).** Do the 3-minute lower routine, then add the buttock stretch (15-20 s per side) and the inner thigh stretch (15-20 s, soles of the feet together, knees lowered gently) [18]. Spend any remaining minute on whichever area feels tightest.

### Rest-day mobility (optional, about 6-8 minutes, Wednesday and one weekend day)

Flexibility exercises are recommended at least twice a week; start gently and build up slowly [19].

| Move | Dose | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Neck rotation | 5 s x 3 per side | Upper back and neck after pressing and pulling days |
| Chest stretch | 20 s x 3 | Front of the shoulders after bench, incline and dips [20] |
| Sideways bend | 2 s x 3 per side | Trunk and lats after rows and pull-ups |
| Calf stretch at the wall | 3 per side | Calves are trained on both lower days |
| Buttock, hamstring and thigh stretches | 15-20 s per side | Hips and legs after squats, deadlifts and split squats |

This routine is light and is not a training session. Skipping it never counts as a missed session. Do not add any loaded training on rest days. In deload weeks, do it on both suggested days.

### Rest days

| Day | Plan | Hours since the same muscles were last trained | Recovery focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Upper A | about 96 h since Upper B (Thursday) | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Tuesday | Lower A | about 96 h since Lower B (Friday) | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Wednesday | Rest | - | Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility |
| Thursday | Upper B | about 72 h since Upper A | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Friday | Lower B | about 72 h since Lower A | Warm-up and cool-down as above |
| Saturday | Rest | - | Everyday walking is fine; optional rest-day mobility (here or Sunday) |
| Sunday | Rest | - | Check your training log and plan the coming week |

Each muscle group gets well over the recommended 48 hours of rest [14]. Walking on rest days is fine; it is everyday movement, not prescribed cardio.

### Sleep and everyday habits

- **Sleep:** adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours a night [15]. Habits that help [15]:
  - go to bed and get up at the same time every day, including at weekends after Friday's session
  - keep the bedroom quiet, relaxing and cool
  - turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime
  - avoid large meals and alcohol before bedtime, and caffeine in the afternoon or evening (this matters if you use caffeinated drinks before late-day sessions)
- **Comfort:** wear loose, comfortable clothing for mobility work and keep water nearby [19].
- **Nutrition:** see section 2. A registered or accredited sports dietitian is the right source [21].

### Care for the joints that work hardest

You reported no limitations, so this care is preventive. It uses only the drills and stretches above.

- **Lower back (deadlift, Romanian deadlift, bent-over row, back squat):** rehearse the hinge in the Friday warm-up and brace on every ramp-up rep. Keep 2 or more reps in reserve on heavy barbell lifts. Pay attention to safety and form [14]. Hamstring and buttock stretches in the cool-down and on rest days help with hinge range.
- **Shoulders (bench, incline press, overhead press, chest dip, pull-up):** do the arm circles, shoulder rolls and thoracic rotations before every upper session, and the dead hang before pull-ups. Use the chest stretch afterwards, and stop at once if it causes pain or tingling in the arms [20]. If the front of the shoulder hurts on dips, use the easier option (close-grip bench press).
- **Knees and ankles (back squat, Bulgarian split squat, leg press):** do ankle rocks and leg swings before lower sessions, and use the 40% squat ramp-up set to check knee tracking. Calf and thigh stretches go in the cool-down. If a knee feels painful rather than just worked, switch to the easier option (goblet squat, rear lunge, shorter leg-press range).
- **General:** if you feel discomfort or pain, contact a health professional before progressing the program [14]. Until then, stop progressing that exercise and use its easier option.

## 9. Tracking and When to Adjust

### What to log

- **Every session:** for each exercise, the load, the reps in every set, and roughly how many reps you had in reserve. The progression rules in section 7 are checked against this log.
- **Recovery notes:** your hours of sleep and a 0-10 soreness rating for each session.
- **Sunday:** review the log and plan the coming week.

### How to judge progress

- **Loads and reps on your main lifts,** and how consistently you train, are the measures of progress. No safe or typical monthly rate of muscle gain was found in the sources, so no target is set, and there are no bodyweight or calorie targets.
- **Pass test at the end of week 5** (section 7): each main lift has at least one load increase, or at least 4 more total reps than in week 1 at the same load.
- **Retest in week 12:** your retest set uses the week-7 starting load. Comparing those reps with week 7 shows your progress on bench press, back squat, pull-up, dumbbell incline press and deadlift.

### Signs to slow down or seek advice

- A set below the rep range, or a barbell lift at 0-1 reps in reserve: follow the hard-set rule (section 7).
- The same lift missing its range 2 sessions in a row: follow the stalled-lift rule.
- Performance falling on several lifts for 2 or more sessions, or clearly worse sleep or soreness: take an extra lighter week. The sleep and soreness notes in your log make this easy to spot.
- Pain, or any of the warning signs in section 2: stop and follow the advice there.

### After week 12

Use your week-12 retest results to set the starting loads for your next cycle. Keep the main lifts and change some accessories again, in line with the advice to vary your routine every 4-8 weeks [14].
