# Evidence-Based Guidelines

## Summary
General exercise information, not medical advice. Adult guidance from [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) and [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity) is at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week plus muscle strengthening on at least 2 days. A beginner can follow a 2-3 day resistance program using 8-12 repetitions and 1-3 sets, per an [IDEA summary of the ACSM 2009 position stand](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults). Mild front-of-knee discomfort is handled by modifying aggravating movements (deep bends, hills, repetitive loading) and not stopping everything, with pain-monitoring rules from an [NHS (RJAH) self-care page](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/). The 3 x 40 minute plan with short cardio finishers will not alone reach 150 minutes, so extra easy walking is suggested as optional.

## Recommended Program Length
8 weeks (provisional length confirmed). Phases:
- Phase 1, weeks 1-3, Foundation: 1-2 sets, pain-free range, learn technique, reps at the upper end of the range with easy effort. Rationale: gradual return and gradual build-up are advised for knee discomfort ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)).
- Phase 2, weeks 4-6, Build: 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps, add reps then load ([IDEA/ACSM 2009](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults)).
- Phase 3, weeks 7-8, Consolidate: hold the best loads, small increases only, and reassess knee comfort. If knee discomfort has not improved after about a month of modified training, the NHS page advises assessment by a GP or physiotherapist ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)). Eight weeks allows that checkpoint and one progression block for a returning beginner.

## Details
### Weekly volume and frequency
- Aerobic: at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, which can be spread across the week in shorter bouts ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html), [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)). Any activity is better than none (both).
- Strength: all major muscle groups on at least 2 days per week ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)). Three strength days meets this.
- Gap: about 30 minutes of cardio from finishers is below 150 minutes, so optional easy walks on non-training days are suggested.

### Strength training dose
- Novices: 2-3 days per week, 8-12 repetitions at about 70-85% of 1RM, 1-3 sets per exercise, 1-2 minutes rest between sets ([IDEA summary of ACSM 2009](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults)). Single-set and multi-set programs give similar early gains for novices (same source).
- [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/) lists at-home options such as sit-to-stands, mini-squats and calf raises, and notes strengthening at least twice weekly.

### Cardio dose
- Moderate intensity such as brisk walking counts toward the 150 minutes ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)). Brief finishers of about 10 minutes are an allocation choice and are not themselves a guideline.

### Recovery
- The sources fetched give no specific rest-day rule beyond the minimum frequency ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/)). The Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday schedule leaves non-training days between most sessions, and this spacing is a planning choice, not a cited requirement.

### Progression and rate of change
- Slowly increase reps first (for example 10 to 12 at the same load), then load ([IDEA summary of ACSM 2009](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults)); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/) also advises gradual rep increases and slower reps.
- Disagreement: the IDEA page reports volume increases of about 2.5-5%. Another summary of the same stand cites 2-10% load increases, but I could not verify that, so I use the conservative range. The ACSM 2009 stand is also a secondary summary here, since the original PDF was unreadable, and ACSM has issued a newer resistance-training stand that I did not verify.

### Limitation-specific safety guidance (mild front-of-knee discomfort)
- Do not stop everything. Reduce aggravating activities at first and return gradually ([RJAH, NHS](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)).
- Typical triggers: repetitive loading, hills, kneeling and squatting, long standing with locked knees (same source).
- If standing knee bends worsen symptoms, start with seated or lying exercises (same source). Examples listed there: static quad, straight leg raise, hip abduction, clam.
- Pain rule: a rise to 2-3/10 that settles quickly is acceptable. Adapt if baseline goes above 4/10 or stays high. If an exercise causes moderate or high pain, reduce or stop it (same source).
- Practical applications to the plan (planner's choice): chair or box squats in a pain-free range, hip-dominant work, flat-ground walking, no impact.

### Red flags to stop and seek care
- Per [RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/): knee locked, giving way with falls, hot red swollen knee, fever with shortness of breath, sudden unexplained weight loss, or no improvement after at least a month. Stop exercising and see a GP or physiotherapist.
- Chest pain or dizziness during exercise: stop and seek medical advice (general precaution, not taken from the fetched pages).
