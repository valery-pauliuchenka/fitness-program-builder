# Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery Plan

## Summary
General exercise information, not medical advice. This plan fits the Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday sessions (40 minutes each: 5 min warm-up + about 25 min strength + 10 min finisher = 40 min, as in 04 and 05). It adds no training time. The warm-up is the 5 minutes already in 04. The cool-down is built into the finisher: the last minute of the walk is done at an easy pace. Extra stretching is optional and is done outside the 40 minutes (later the same day, or in the rest-day block). Longer mobility work is placed on rest days (Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday) as optional 10-minute blocks, which can sit alongside the optional easy walks from 05. Knee care follows the pain rules in 04 and 05: a rise to 2-3/10 that settles quickly is acceptable; above 4/10 or staying high means use an easier option or stop.

## Details
### Warm-up
Standard 5-minute warm-up, same for sessions A, B and C (matches 04). Do each item in a pain-free range.
1. Marching on the spot or a brisk walk around the room: 2 min, easy.
2. Hip circles and leg swings holding a chair, small range: 8 each side (30 s).
3. Cat-cow on the mat (or seated, if kneeling bothers the knees): 6 slow reps.
4. Arm circles and band pull-aparts with a light band: 10 each (45 s). Adds shoulder prep for push and row work.
5. Ramp-up: 1 light set (about half load) of the first exercise of the day, 6-8 reps.
Session-specific note: on Tuesday and Saturday (romanian deadlift first) add 6 hip hinges with no weight; on Thursday (supported squat first) add 6 chair sit-to-stands to a comfortable height. Keep the whole warm-up to 5 minutes by shortening item 1 if needed.

### Cool-down and mobility
Cool-down inside the 40 minutes: walk the final minute of the 10-minute finisher at an easy pace (RPE 2-3) and breathe slowly, so heart rate settles without extra time.

Optional stretching, done after the session ends (outside the 40 minutes, for 3-5 minutes, skip it if time is short). Hold each 20-30 seconds, breathe slowly, stay at mild tension and never into pain.
- Standing calf stretch against a wall, 1 each side.
- Hamstring stretch with the leg resting on a low chair or step, straight back, 1 each side.
- Figure-four or seated glute stretch on a chair, 1 each side.
- Chest stretch in a doorway or with arms behind the back, 1 time.
- Child's pose on the mat for the back and hips, only if kneeling is comfortable; otherwise lie on the back with knees supported.

Optional 10-minute mobility block on a rest day (not required): the warm-up items 2-4, a 30-second hold of each stretch above, and a supported thoracic stretch (lie on a rolled towel along the spine, arms open) for the desk-worker posture. Keep it easy; it is not training.

### Rest days, deloads and missed sessions
- Weekly layout: Tue train, Wed rest, Thu train, Fri rest, Sat train, Sun rest, Mon rest. Wednesday and Friday give one day between sessions, and the long gap is Sunday and Monday. Keep this spacing; do not train on consecutive days.
- Rest-day activity: easy walking of 15-20 minutes (the optional walks in 05, RPE 2-3), light mobility, and normal daily movement. Break up long sitting periods with a short walk or stand every 30-60 minutes. If the knee feels sore the day after a session, choose flat walking or rest rather than stairs or hills.
- Soreness: mild muscle soreness for 1-2 days after starting or increasing load is normal. Joint pain, especially at the front of the knee, that is above 4/10 or lingers is not, and means easier options at the next session.
- Deload (consistent with 04): no scheduled deload in 8 weeks for a beginner. In week 7, drop 1 set per exercise only if the last two sessions felt very hard or knee pain rose above 4/10. This is a planning choice, as 02 gives no cited deload rule.
- Hard session: repeat the same loads next session and use the easier option for any painful exercise.
- Missed 1 session: do it on the next free day if a rest day follows it; otherwise skip it. Do not double up. Continue at the same loads.
- Missed several sessions or a break up to 1 week: resume at the previous loads. After more than 1 week away, drop loads by about 10% and 1 set for the first session (as in 04).
- Illness: skip training while you have a fever or feel unwell below the neck; return gradually once you feel well, using the missed-session rules.

### Sleep and recovery habits
- Aim for a regular sleep schedule with about 7 or more hours a night for adults ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). Keep similar bed and wake times, including on training days.
- Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool, and avoid screens and caffeine late in the evening.
- Stay hydrated through the day by drinking water to thirst.
- Manage stress with short breaks, slow breathing for 2-3 minutes, or an easy walk.
- Keep a short log of loads, reps and knee comfort (0-10) after each session. Use it for the week 4 checkpoint and week 8 reassessment in 04.
- For eating or nutrition questions, a registered dietitian is the right source. This plan gives no diet advice.

### Limitation-specific care
Limitation: mild front-of-knee discomfort with deep squats and stair descent (no swelling, no giving way). Guidance follows self-care advice from the NHS trust source ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)).
- Mobility choices avoid deep knee flexion and kneeling under pressure: use standing calf, hamstring and glute stretches, supported hip circles, and lying or seated options in place of deep lunges, deep squats or sitting on the heels.
- Quad stretch, if used, is only to mild tension in standing, holding a chair, and only if it does not provoke front-of-knee pain; skip it otherwise.
- Warm-up sit-to-stands stay at or above chair height. Never go deeper than in the training plan.
- On rest days, walk on flat routes and avoid long stair descents. Take the lift or use the handrail and go slowly where stairs are unavoidable.
- Pad the knees with the mat for kneeling moves, or use the alternatives listed in 04 (dead bug in place of the kneeling plank tap).
- Pacing: after a session, mild knee awareness that settles within 24 hours is acceptable. If knee discomfort has not improved after about a month, see a GP or physiotherapist, as set out in 04.
- Ice or heat is optional comfort only and not a treatment; do not use it to push through pain.

### Red flags
Stop the activity and seek professional advice (a GP, physiotherapist or emergency services as appropriate) if you have:
- Sharp or sudden pain, or pain that rises above 4/10 and stays high.
- Knee swelling, heat or redness, locking, catching or giving way.
- Dizziness, fainting, unusual breathlessness, or palpitations.
- Chest pain, pressure or discomfort, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw (call emergency services).
- Numbness, tingling, or pain that spreads down a limb.
- Calf pain with swelling after a long period of inactivity.
- Pain that wakes you at night or does not ease after about a month of modified training.
This is general information and does not replace medical advice.
