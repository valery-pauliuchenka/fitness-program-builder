# Training Plan

## Summary
An 8-week, 3-day-per-week full-body home program (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, 40 minutes each) for a returning beginner with mild front-of-knee discomfort. Each session is a 5-minute warm-up, about 25 minutes of strength work (5 exercises) and a 10-minute low-impact cardio finisher, which follows the session allocation in 01. Phases follow 02: Foundation (weeks 1-3), Build (weeks 4-6), Consolidate (weeks 7-8). Lower-body work is hip-dominant; squatting is limited to chair height; there is no impact. Weekly pushing and pulling are balanced (3 push and 3 pull exercises per week). All exercises come from the 03 pool, with no swaps. General exercise information, not medical advice.

## Details
### Structure
- Length: 8 weeks, 3 phases (per 02 "Recommended Program Length").
- Strength frequency: 3 days, which meets the 2+ days guideline ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)).
- Dose: 1-3 sets, 8-12 reps, 1-2 minutes rest for novices ([IDEA/ACSM 2009](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults)).
- Time budget per session: warm-up 5 min + strength about 25 min (10-12 working sets at roughly 2 min each, including rest) + cardio finisher 10 min = 40 min.
- Warm-up (all sessions, 5 min): easy marching on the spot or a brisk walk around the room, shoulder and hip circles, then 1 light ramp-up set of the first exercise. Cardio finisher: 10 min brisk flat-ground walking (or easy cycling if available) at a pace where you can talk; details are in the cardio plan. No hills or stairs down.
- Rest between sets: 60-90 s (up to 2 min on the main lifts).

| Day | Session | Duration |
|---|---|---|

| Thursday | B: squat, bridge, press, row, core | 40 min |
| Saturday | C: hinge, push, rear delt, core, calves | 40 min |
| Mon, Wed, Fri, Sun | Rest (optional easy walk, see 02 on the 150-minute gap) | n/a |

Weekly muscle coverage: glutes and hamstrings (A, B, C), quads (A, B at chair height), chest and triceps (A, C, press on B), upper back (A, B, C), rear delts (C), abs (A, B, C), calves (C).

### Sessions
Effort: Phase 1 at an easy effort (about 3 reps left in reserve), later phases 1-2 reps left in reserve. Knee rule for every exercise: a rise to 2-3/10 that settles quickly is fine; if it goes above 4/10 or stays high, use the easier option or stop ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)).

## Progression
General rule ([IDEA/ACSM 2009](https://www.ideafit.com/progression-models-in-resistance-training-for-healthy-adults), [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/)): add reps first, then load, with small steps (the conservative range from 02, about 2.5-5%). Dumbbells are adjustable, so use the smallest step available (about 1 kg, or 2 kg total on two-dumbbell lifts).

**Phase 1 (weeks 1-3, Foundation)**
- Keep 2 sets per exercise at an easy effort. Learn technique and a pain-free range.
- Week to week: add 1 rep per set while staying within the written rep range. When all sets reach the top of the range (12) with good form and knee pain at or below 2-3/10, increase load by the smallest step in the next session and drop back to the bottom of the range.
- Step-ups in difficulty (incline to kneeling to push-up, band to dumbbell row, bridge to bridge march) happen only when the top of the range is reached on all sets for 2 sessions in a row.

**Phase 2 (weeks 4-6, Build)**
- Move main lifts to 3 sets (accessories stay at 2). Same double progression: reach 12 reps on every set in 2 consecutive sessions, then add the smallest load step.
- Squat depth never increases. Progress the supported squat by load or a slower tempo only.
- Week 4 checkpoint: if knee discomfort has not improved after about a month of modified training, arrange a GP or physiotherapist assessment ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)), and keep to the easier options meanwhile.

**Phase 3 (weeks 7-8, Consolidate)**
- Keep the sets from Phase 2. Hold the best loads; at most one small load step per exercise across the 2 weeks.
- Week 8 reassessment: note the loads and reps reached, and knee comfort at the chair squat and on stairs, to plan the next block.

**Deload**
- The guidelines in 02 give no cited deload rule. As a planning choice: reduce each exercise by 1 set in week 7 if the last two sessions felt very hard, or pain rose above 4/10. Otherwise do not deload in an 8-week program for a beginner.

**Hard or missed sessions**
- Hard session (could not finish the reps, or knee pain above 4/10 that stays high): repeat the same loads next session; use the easier option for the painful exercise.
- Missed 1 session: do it on the next free day if at least one rest day follows; otherwise skip it. Do not double up. Continue at the same loads.
- Missed 2 or more sessions in a row, or a break of up to 1 week: resume at the loads from before the break. After more than 1 week away, drop loads by about 10% and 1 set for the first session.
- Never train through knee swelling, locking or giving way. Stop and see a GP or physiotherapist ([RJAH](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/anterior-knee-pain/)). Chest pain or dizziness: stop and seek medical advice.
