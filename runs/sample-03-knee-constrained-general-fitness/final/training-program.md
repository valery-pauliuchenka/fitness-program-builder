# 8-Week Knee-Friendly Home Strength and Fitness Program for a Returning Beginner

A 3-day-per-week, 40-minute full-body plan with a short low-impact walking finisher, built around mild front-of-knee discomfort.

## 1. Overview
- Goal: general fitness and strength, with no deadline or numeric target.
- Length: 8 weeks, in three phases (Foundation weeks 1-3, Build weeks 4-6, Consolidate weeks 7-8).
- Sessions: 3 per week (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday), 40 minutes each.
- Equipment: adjustable dumbbells (2-20 kg), a resistance band, a mat and a sturdy chair.

Each session is a 5-minute warm-up, about 25 minutes of strength work (5 exercises) and a 10-minute low-impact walking finisher. Lower-body work centres on hip-dominant moves (hinges and bridges). Squats stop at chair height and there is no jumping or impact. Eight weeks gives a returning beginner time to adapt, work through one progression block and check how the knee is responding after about a month of modified training.

## 2. Safety Notice
This is general exercise information, not medical advice.

- Stop any exercise that increases your knee pain. If discomfort worsens, see a doctor or physiotherapist.
- Stop and get medical advice for chest pain, dizziness, fainting or unusual breathlessness. Call emergency services for chest pain, pressure, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw.
- Your mild front-of-knee discomfort (deep squats, walking downstairs; no swelling, no giving way, no recent injury) was judged suitable for a modified program. Adaptations made for it:
  - Squats are limited to chair height, and depth is never increased.
  - Hip-dominant exercises lead the lower body.
  - No impact or jumping.
  - Cardio is flat-ground walking (or easy cycling), with no hills and no long stair descents.
  - Kneeling moves are padded with the mat and have alternatives.
- Knee pain rule: a rise to 2-3 out of 10 that settles quickly is fine. If it goes above 4 out of 10 or stays high, use the easier option or stop.
- See a GP or physiotherapist if your knee locks, gives way, becomes hot, red or swollen, or if discomfort has not improved after about a month.
- Other reasons to stop and seek advice: sharp or sudden pain, numbness or tingling, calf pain with swelling after long inactivity, or pain that wakes you at night.
- This program contains no diet or supplement advice.

## 4. Program at a Glance
| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Intensity guidance | Key changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Foundation | 1-3 | Learn technique, pain-free range, gradual return | About 3 reps left in reserve; walk at RPE 3-4 (full conversation) | 2 sets per exercise; walk 8, 8, then 10 minutes |
| 2 Build | 4-6 | Add reps, then load | 1-2 reps left in reserve; walk at RPE 4-5 | Main lifts go to 3 sets; Thursday walk adds 1-minute brisk intervals; week 4 knee checkpoint |
| 3 Consolidate | 7-8 | Hold best loads, small increases only, reassess | 1-2 reps left in reserve; walk at RPE 5 (intervals up to RPE 5-6) | Thursday brisk intervals lengthen to 2 minutes; week 8 reassessment |

RPE is a 1-10 effort scale. RPE 3-4 means you can hold a full conversation. RPE 5-6 means short sentences only. Do not go above RPE 6.

Every session is 5 min warm-up + about 25 min strength + 10 min walking finisher. The layout is the same in all phases. What changes is the number of sets, the walking duration and effort, and the length of the optional rest-day walks (15 minutes in Phase 1, 20 minutes in Phases 2-3). The three sessions give about 24-30 minutes of cardio a week, below the 150 minutes of moderate activity recommended for adults. The optional easy walks on rest days help close that gap.

## 7. Progression Rules
General approach: add reps first, then load, in small steps (about 2.5-5%). With adjustable dumbbells, use the smallest step available (about 1 kg, or 2 kg total on two-dumbbell lifts).

- Phase 1: keep 2 sets at an easy effort. Add 1 rep per set each week within the written rep range. When every set reaches the top of the range (12) with good form and knee pain at or below 2-3 out of 10, increase the load by the smallest step next session and drop back to the bottom of the range.
- Harder versions (incline to kneeling to full push-up, band to dumbbell row, bridge to bridge march) only when you reach the top of the range on all sets for 2 sessions in a row.
- Phase 2: main lifts go to 3 sets (accessories stay at 2). Same rule: reach 12 reps on every set in 2 consecutive sessions, then add the smallest load step.
- Squat depth never increases. Progress the supported squat by load or a slower tempo only.
- Week 4 checkpoint: if your knee has not improved after about a month of modified training, arrange a GP or physiotherapist assessment and keep to the easier options meanwhile.
- Phase 3: keep the Phase 2 sets, hold your best loads, and make at most one small load step per exercise across the 2 weeks. In week 8, note the loads and reps reached and how the knee feels at the chair squat and on stairs, to plan your next block.
- Walking: change one thing at a time (duration, then intensity), as laid out in the finisher table. If your knee is above the pain limit, repeat the previous week.
- Deload: none is scheduled for a beginner in 8 weeks. In week 7, drop 1 set per exercise only if your last two sessions felt very hard or knee pain rose above 4 out of 10. This is a planning choice.
- Hard session (could not finish the reps, or knee pain above 4 out of 10 that stays high): repeat the same loads next session and use the easier option for the painful exercise.
- Missed 1 session: do it on the next free day if a rest day follows it; otherwise skip it. Never double up. Continue at the same loads.
- Missed 2 or more sessions, or a break of up to 1 week: resume at your previous loads. After more than 1 week away, drop loads by about 10% and 1 set for the first session.
- Never train through knee swelling, locking or giving way. Stop and see a GP or physiotherapist.
- Illness: skip training while you have a fever or feel unwell below the neck, then return gradually using the missed-session rules.

## 8. Warm-up, Mobility and Recovery
### Warm-up (5 minutes, every session, pain-free range)
1. Marching on the spot or a brisk walk around the room: 2 min, easy.
2. Hip circles and leg swings holding a chair, small range: 8 each side (30 s).
3. Cat-cow on the mat (or seated if kneeling bothers the knees): 6 slow reps.
4. Arm circles and band pull-aparts with a light band: 10 each (45 s).
5. Ramp-up: 1 light set (about half load) of the first exercise, 6-8 reps.

On Tuesday and Saturday (deadlift first) add 6 hip hinges with no weight. On Thursday (squat first) add 6 chair sit-to-stands to a comfortable height. Shorten item 1 if needed to stay at 5 minutes.

### Cool-down and mobility
- Cool-down is built in: walk the final minute of the finisher easily and breathe slowly.
- Optional stretching after the session (3-5 minutes, outside the 40 minutes). Hold each 20-30 seconds at mild tension, never into pain:
  - Standing calf stretch against a wall, 1 each side.
  - Hamstring stretch with the leg resting on a low chair, straight back, 1 each side.
  - Figure-four or seated glute stretch on a chair, 1 each side.
  - Chest stretch in a doorway or with arms behind the back, once.
  - Child's pose on the mat only if kneeling is comfortable; otherwise lie on your back with knees supported.
- Optional 10-minute rest-day mobility block: warm-up items 2-4, a 30-second hold of each stretch above, and a supported thoracic stretch (lie on a rolled towel along the spine, arms open) for desk posture. Keep it easy; it is not training.

### Rest days
- Rest-day activity: easy walking of 15-20 minutes (RPE 2-3), light mobility and normal daily movement. Break up long sitting every 30-60 minutes with a short walk or stand.
- Mild muscle soreness for 1-2 days after starting or increasing load is normal. Joint pain at the front of the knee that is above 4 out of 10 or lingers is not; use easier options at the next session.
- If the knee feels sore the day after a session, choose flat walking or rest rather than stairs or hills.

### Sleep and general habits
- Aim for a regular sleep schedule of about 7 or more hours a night, with similar bed and wake times, including on training days.
- Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool, and avoid screens and caffeine late in the evening.
- Drink water to thirst through the day.
- Manage stress with short breaks, 2-3 minutes of slow breathing, or an easy walk.
- For eating or nutrition questions, a registered dietitian is the right source.

### Knee care
- Mobility choices avoid deep knee flexion and kneeling under pressure. Use standing or seated options instead of deep lunges, deep squats or sitting on your heels.
- A quad stretch, if used, is standing, holding a chair, to mild tension only, and skipped if it provokes front-of-knee pain.
- Warm-up sit-to-stands stay at or above chair height.
- On rest days walk on flat routes and avoid long stair descents. Use the lift or handrail and go slowly where stairs are unavoidable.
- Pad the knees with the mat for kneeling moves, or use the alternative (dead bug in place of the kneeling plank tap).
- After a session, mild knee awareness that settles within 24 hours is acceptable.
- Ice or heat is optional comfort only, not a treatment; do not use it to push through pain.

## 9. Tracking and When to Adjust
- Log after each session: loads, reps, and knee comfort (0-10). Use the log for the week 4 checkpoint and the week 8 reassessment.
- Progress means more reps or a small load increase at the same knee comfort, steadier technique, and walking that feels easier at the same pace.
- Slow down (repeat the week, use easier options) if knee pain rises above 4 out of 10 or stays high, if you cannot finish the reps, or if you feel unusually worn out.
- Stop and seek professional advice for any of the red flags listed in section 2, and for sharp or sudden pain, swelling, heat, redness, locking, catching or giving way.
- Week 4: check whether the knee has improved. If not after about a month of modified training, see a GP or physiotherapist.
- Week 8: record your best loads and reps and how the knee feels at the chair squat and on stairs. Use these to plan your next block. If you re-plan, keep the same pain rules and add changes gradually.
